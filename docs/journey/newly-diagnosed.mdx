Hearing the diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease can be overwhelming. It is completely normal to feel a range of emotions, including anger, fear, frustration, and sadness. You are not alone, and there are concrete steps you can take to plan for the future.

## 1. Process Your Emotions

Give yourself and your family time to digest the news. 
- Talk to a counselor or clergy member.
- Join a support group for people in the early stages of Alzheimer’s.
- Call the Alzheimer's Association helpline (800-272-3900) for emotional support.

## 2. Get Organized (Legal and Financial)

It is crucial to handle legal and financial planning while the diagnosed individual can still make decisions.
- **Power of Attorney (POA):** Designate a trusted person to make financial and health care decisions.
- **Living Will:** Outline end-of-life care preferences.
- **Financial Planning:** Consult an elder care attorney to understand Medicaid, Medicare, and long-term care insurance.

## 3. Understand the Treatment Options

While there is currently no cure, there are medications that can help manage symptoms or potentially slow the disease progression in its early stages.
- Discuss medications like Leqembi (lecanemab) or Kisunla (donanemab) with your neurologist.
- Learn more about how these treatments work in our [Guide to Amyloid Plaques](/journey/amyloid-hypothesis-consumer).
- Review eligibility for clinical trials. See our [Clinical Trials Guide](/trials) for simple explanations of ongoing research.

## 4. Build a Care Team

Alzheimer's is not a journey you should take alone.
- **Medical:** Neurologists, geriatricians, and social workers.
- **Personal:** Friends, family, and neighbors. Be specific when they ask how they can help (e.g., "Can you bring dinner on Tuesdays?").
