## Why it matters

The A4 trial was designed to test whether early intervention with a monomer-targeting antibody could prevent disease progression. By testing solanezumab in individuals with elevated amyloid but no cognitive impairment, it aimed to answer whether stopping amyloid accumulation before symptoms arise is sufficient to delay Alzheimer's.

## Results

Solanezumab failed to slow cognitive decline over 240 weeks (mean PACC score change -1.43 vs -1.13 for placebo, p=0.26). Amyloid continued to accumulate in both groups, though slightly less in the treatment arm. The trial confirmed that targeting monomeric amyloid in preclinical stages does not provide clinical benefit.

## Status log

- **2014-02-01** — Trial begins enrollment.
- **2017-06-01** — Protocol amended to quadruple the solanezumab dose from 400 mg to 1600 mg based on external trial data.
- **2022-12-01** — Trial completes clinical phase.
- **2023-07-17** — Primary results published in NEJM showing no clinical benefit ([Sperling et al., 2023](/research/papers/solanezumab-a4-prevention)).
