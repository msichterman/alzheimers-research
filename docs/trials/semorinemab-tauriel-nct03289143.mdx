## Why it matters

Tauriel was designed to test whether targeting the N-terminal domain of the tau protein with semorinemab could slow or stop the spread of tau pathology and clinical decline in early Alzheimer's disease. Its failure raised critical questions about the viability of N-terminal anti-tau immunotherapies.

## Results

The trial completely failed to show clinical efficacy on its primary and secondary endpoints. Semorinemab did not slow cognitive or functional decline, nor did it reduce cerebral tau accumulation on PET scans. Due to the lack of efficacy, the trial's open-label extension was terminated.
