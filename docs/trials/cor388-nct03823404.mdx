## Why it matters

GAIN was the definitive human test of the P. gingivalis/gingipain hypothesis described in [this review](/research/papers/p-gingivalis-cor388-alzheimers): if gingipain inhibition slowed cognitive decline, it would satisfy the review's 'treatment efficacy' criterion for causation. It's the largest trial to date of an anti-infective mechanism in Alzheimer's.

## Results

GAIN missed both co-primary endpoints at 48 weeks. A subgroup the sponsor described as pre-specified (roughly half the cohort, positive for oral P. gingivalis DNA) showed a dose-response signal suggesting slowed decline, but this was not a confirmed primary result and was never independently replicated. Liver enzyme elevations occurred in 15% of high-dose and 7% of low-dose patients vs. 2% on placebo, prompting an FDA clinical hold and the program's full discontinuation in 2022. See [Sabbagh & Decourt, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10275298/) for a fuller account.

## Status log

- **2019-03** — Trial begins, enrolling 643 patients with mild-to-moderate AD
- **2021-10** — Topline results: fails both co-primary endpoints (ADAS-Cog11, ADCS-ADL); dose-dependent liver enzyme elevations noted ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/cortexyme-alzheimers-gain-study-results-gingivalis/608879/))
- **2022-01** — FDA places a clinical hold on atuzaginstat over hepatotoxicity ([press release](https://ir.quincetx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/cortexyme-announces-clinical-trial-hold-atuzaginstats-investigational))
- **2022-08** — Cortexyme discontinues the gingipain-inhibitor program and rebrands as Quince Therapeutics ([FierceBiotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/quince-hoping-sweeten-its-fruit-new-strategic-plan-acquisitions-and-out-licensing-legacy))
