**Source**: [JAMA Neurology, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2022.1375) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35696185/)
**Authors**: Edmond Teng et al. — Genentech, Inc.

## Takeaway

The Phase 2 Tauriel trial found that the N-terminal anti-tau monoclonal antibody semorinemab did not slow clinical decline or cerebral tau accumulation in patients with prodromal to mild Alzheimer's disease. While biomarker data indicated the drug successfully engaged its target in plasma and CSF, these biological effects did not translate into clinical benefit.

## Key findings

- Semorinemab yielded no significant clinical benefit on the primary endpoint (CDR-SB), with the placebo group declining by 2.19 points and treatment groups declining by 2.36 to 2.41 points over 73 weeks (paper, Results).
- The drug failed to reduce cerebral tau accumulation, as measured by [18F]GTP1 tau PET imaging, with all arms showing similar increases (paper, Results).
- Dose-dependent increases in plasma total tau and modest declines in CSF tau indices indicated the drug engaged its target in both the periphery and central nervous system (paper, Results).
- The antibody demonstrated an acceptable safety profile, with similar overall rates of adverse events between the placebo (93.1%) and semorinemab (88.8% to 94.7%) arms (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial across 97 sites. Enrolled 457 individuals aged 50-80 with prodromal to mild Alzheimer's disease and confirmed amyloid pathology. Participants received intravenous placebo or semorinemab (1500 mg, 4500 mg, or 8100 mg) for 73 weeks. Trial registered as NCT03289143.

## Trial evidence

Tauriel ([NCT03289143](/trials/semorinemab-tauriel-nct03289143)) provided definitive evidence that targeting the N-terminus of tau with semorinemab does not slow early Alzheimer's disease. A parallel Phase 2 trial in a moderate AD population, [Lauriet](/research/papers/semorinemab-lauriet), was also formally negative on its functional coprimary endpoint. Genentech subsequently discontinued clinical development of the drug.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is exceptionally quiet among patient and caregiver communities, as the drug failed its efficacy endpoints and development was halted. Within the scientific community, semorinemab is critically discussed as a pivotal case study illustrating the disconnect between biological target engagement (lowering CSF tau) and clinical outcomes.

## Caveats

- The study relied on remote clinical assessments for some participants after March 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, though sensitivity analyses found this did not alter the primary outcomes.
- Small sample sizes for longitudinal CSF data limited the power of post hoc analyses on tau biomarker changes.
- The trial population was predominantly White (91.9%), restricting the generalizability of the findings to more diverse populations.
