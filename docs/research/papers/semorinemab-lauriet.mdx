## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: not_met.** The two coprimary endpoints split: the ADAS-Cog11 showed a 42.2% reduction in cognitive decline at week 49 (−2.89 points, 95% CI −4.56 to −1.21, p = 0.0008) while the functional coprimary ADCS-ADL showed no treatment effect (−0.83 points, 95% CI −3.39 to 1.72, p = 0.52), and the document itself concludes that on the prespecified coprimary endpoints the study was negative.
> Based on the results of the prespecified coprimary endpoints, this study was negative. While semorinemab had a significant effect on cognition measured by the ADAS-Cog11, this effect did not extend to improved functional or global outcomes.
**Multiplicity handling:** none stated — the document describes no multiple-comparison adjustment; it reports a 2-sided α = 0.2 significance level for the sample size calculation, coprimary endpoints requiring both cognitive and functional measures, and a prespecified interim futility analysis at approximately 40% completion

### Risk of bias

- **sponsor conduct, authorship and analysis** — The study was funded by Genentech, Inc., and every listed author is an employee of Genentech and a shareholder in F. Hoffmann La Roche; sponsor staff performed the statistical analysis and study supervision, and the sponsor formed the data monitoring committee.
- **liberal type I error rate / multiplicity** — The sample size calculation used a 2-sided α = 0.2 significance level, far more permissive than conventional α = 0.05, and the document describes no adjustment for the multiple endpoints, two time points (week 49 and week 61) and three subgroup analyses reported.
- **attrition and protocol change during conduct** — The overall discontinuation rate during the placebo-controlled period was 24%, and 34 of 272 randomized participants were excluded from the mITT population (272 randomized, 238 analysed); the protocol was amended mid-study in April 2020 to create two cohorts with different treatment durations.
- **COVID-19 disruption of drug exposure** — Between March 2020 and the end of the double-blind period, 110 participants missed a total of 397 doses, with 45 cohort 2 participants missing 3 or more doses; the document argues serum exposure was negligibly affected, and analyses in the main manuscript use the mITT rather than the restricted mITT population.
- **isolated positive endpoint / risk of false-positive** — The document itself raises that the single positive coprimary result, driven post hoc by one item of one domain, may be a false positive given the absence of effects on all other clinical endpoints.
- **generalizability** — Participants were predominantly non-Hispanic White (White: 109/123 semorinemab, 101/115 placebo), and the document states the lack of racial and ethnic diversity and the relatively small sample size may restrict generalizability.
- **remote outcome assessment** — Because of the COVID-19 pandemic, CDR and/or ADCS-ADL assessments could be performed by telephone, introducing heterogeneity in the administration of the functional coprimary and a secondary endpoint; the document does not report how many assessments were remote.

### Limitations stated by the document

- The COVID-19 pandemic contributed to missed doses and/or remote or missed clinical assessments for some participants; there might be unknown effects of the pandemic on the results that were not assessed by the preplanned sensitivity analyses
- The relatively small sample size may restrict the generalizability of the findings
- The lack of racial and ethnic diversity in the study cohort may restrict the generalizability of the findings
- The observed ADAS-Cog11 treatment effect, given the absence of treatment effects on any of the other clinical endpoints, may represent a false-positive result
- The possible cognitive benefit did not extend to improved functional or global outcomes and therefore may not meet the established regulatory standards for a disease-modifying drug for AD

### Where authoritative sources disagree

*Stored as structured evidence with each position attributed to its source, not resolved into a single verdict.*

- **Was the Lauriet study positive or negative on its coprimary endpoints?** — Negative. Despite a statistically significant ADAS-Cog11 result, the document states that on the prespecified coprimary endpoints the study was negative because the cognitive effect did not extend to functional or global outcomes. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
- **Does semorinemab slow functional decline in mild-to-moderate AD?** — No — the document classifies its own finding as Class I evidence that semorinemab does not slow functional decline in patients with mild-to-moderate AD. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document), under the journal's Classification of Evidence scheme)*
- **Is the observed ADAS-Cog11 benefit real or a false positive?** — The document holds both readings open: it argues the effect was consistent across subgroups, at week 61 and in the restricted mITT population, while explicitly stating the effect may represent a false-positive result given the absence of effects on every other clinical endpoint. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
- **Might N-terminal tau-targeted antibodies be more effective at later disease stages than in prodromal-to-mild AD?** — The document raises this as a possibility, noting Tauriel, gosuranemab, tilavonemab and zagotenemab all failed in prodromal-to-mild AD and none of the other molecules have been studied in mild-to-moderate AD. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
- **Does semorinemab work through the mechanism originally hypothesised (blocking tau spread and NFT accumulation)?** — No. Semorinemab had no effect on NFT accumulation measured by tau PET or on cerebral atrophy, so the document proposes instead that any benefit may come from reducing soluble toxic tau species — a revision of the original therapeutic hypothesis. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
- **Do the different results of Tauriel and Lauriet reflect the disease stage of the populations studied?** — The document proposes that the more severe baseline cognitive and functional deficits in Lauriet relative to Tauriel might explain why the two studies yielded different results. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
- **Should semorinemab or other anti-tau therapies be developed further in this population?** — The document argues the results may warrant further exploration of semorinemab or other anti-tau therapies in mild-to-moderate AD, including combination with anti-Aβ antibodies, while conceding the cognitive benefit may not meet regulatory standards for a disease-modifying drug. *(Monteiro et al., the Lauriet investigators (this document))*
