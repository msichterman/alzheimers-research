**Authors**: Ovod, V. et al. — Washington University School of Medicine

## Takeaway

This prospective study establishes that the ratio of Aβ42 to Aβ40 in blood plasma is significantly lower in individuals with brain amyloidosis. Utilizing precise mass spectrometry, the plasma assay achieved high diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.8865), providing the technological foundation for commercial clinical blood tests like PrecivityAD.

## Key findings

- Plasma Aβ isoforms turn over rapidly with a half-life of approximately 3 hours, compared to the previously established ~9-hour half-life in cerebrospinal fluid (paper, Results).
- The plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 concentration ratio was an average of 14.3% lower in amyloid-positive participants compared to those without amyloidosis (paper, Results).
- A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis of the absolute plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio demonstrated strong diagnostic accuracy for central nervous system amyloidosis with an AUC of 0.8865 (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Prospective study of 41 participants over age 60 (23 amyloid negative, 18 amyloid positive). Researchers utilized a Stable Isotope Labeling Kinetics (SILK) protocol with hourly blood sampling over 24 hours. Plasma results were anchored against prior PIB-PET imaging or CSF Aβ markers.

## Trial evidence

This study reports foundational findings from [NCT02021682](/trials/cns-plasma-abeta-kinetics-nct02021682), which ultimately enrolled 58 participants. The high-precision mass-spectrometry approach pioneered here formed the basis for the commercial PrecivityAD test. The clinical utility of this diagnostic approach was later validated in real-world settings by the QUIP I trial ([NCT05477056](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05477056)).

## Public sentiment

Public and media sentiment is highly enthusiastic about the transition from invasive lumbar punctures and expensive PET scans to accessible blood tests. However, patient communities express significant confusion regarding how to interpret complex ratio cutoffs and Amyloid Probability Scores, occasionally overestimating the test's ability to definitively predict the clinical onset of dementia.

## Caveats

- The initial sample size was small (N=41), serving as a proof-of-concept that required the subsequent large-scale, multi-cohort validations that followed in later years.
- For some participants, ground-truth amyloid status was determined via PET scan up to several years prior to the blood draw. This temporal gap introduces the possibility that some individuals characterized as 'false positives' on the blood test had actually developed amyloidosis in the interim.
