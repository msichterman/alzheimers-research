**Authors**: Klunk et al. — University of Pittsburgh / Avid Radiopharmaceuticals (Eli Lilly)

## Takeaway

The Centiloid scale provides a universal 0-100 metric for amyloid PET scans, anchoring 0 to young, healthy brains and 100 to typical Alzheimer's disease. This standard allows clinical trials and researchers to compare plaque clearance directly, regardless of the specific radiotracer or imaging hardware used.

## Key findings

- Anchored a 0-100 scale using young controls (≤45 years) for 0 and typical mild-to-moderate AD patients for 100 (paper, Section 1.3.2).
- Identified the whole cerebellum as the optimal standard reference region due to its low signal variance in healthy controls (paper, Section 1.3.1).
- Demonstrated that different tracers and analysis methods can be reliably scaled to Centiloids, achieving a 0.991 correlation with standard PiB methods in a validation subset (paper, Section 2.5).

## Methods in brief

Retrospective methodological study analyzing 125 subjects: 34 young controls (≤45 years), 47 typical mild-to-moderate AD patients, and 44 additional subjects for region-of-interest definition. The project developed a standard Level-1 baseline method using PiB-PET and a Level-2 calibration process for non-standard tracers.

## Trial evidence

The Centiloid scale has become the foundational metric for measuring amyloid clearance in modern Phase 3 clinical trials. Pivotal trials like CLARITY AD (lecanemab) and [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2](/trials/trailblazer-alz-2-nct04437511) have utilized Centiloids not just as secondary endpoints, but to define strict threshold criteria for clinical outcomes and stopping patient dosing (e.g., &lt;11 Centiloids for clearance).

## Public sentiment

The scientific and clinical research communities enthusiastically treat Centiloids as the definitive common language for amyloid quantification. However, this metric remains almost entirely absent from public and patient discourse, where conversations focus firmly on top-line cognitive benefits and drug brand names.

## Caveats

- The scale's anchor points were established using cross-sectional data, meaning longitudinal performance across different reference regions requires further validation.
- The standardization method does not completely account for variance caused by substantial differences in scanner hardware, reconstruction algorithms, or attenuation correction protocols across imaging sites.
- Applying Centiloid scaling to small, specific brain regions (like the precuneus) rather than the global cortex can distort the numerical meaning of the 0-100 scale.
