## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: not_applicable.** This document is a secondary safety analysis whose main outcome measure was MRI monitoring for ARIA with associated symptoms reported as adverse events; it explicitly states that the primary analysis of the trials will be reported elsewhere, so it reports no primary efficacy endpoint of its own.
> This report includes secondary analyses of the trials. The primary analysis will be reported elsewhere. Most of the secondary analyses for this report were prespecified for the integrated summary of safety but were not included in the trial protocol.
**Multiplicity handling:** none stated

### Risk of bias

- **unblinding via ARIA** — The document acknowledges the potential for functional unblinding due to ARIA and states it was addressed in the study design by separating efficacy raters from safety raters and keeping efficacy raters blinded to ARIA assessments.
- **attrition / early termination** — 44.8% of the safety population did not complete the placebo-controlled period, predominantly because the sponsor terminated the studies early, which the document itself says could affect the incidence of adverse events.
- **prespecification of analyses** — The analyses in this report were not in the trial protocol; most were prespecified only in the integrated summary of safety, which is not the protocol-level prespecification a reader might assume.
- **ad hoc analyses included** — The document states that some analyses in this report are ad hoc analyses performed to address regulatory queries, so they are not prespecified.
- **selection — baseline exclusion of hemorrhage-prone participants** — Participants with more than 4 baseline microhemorrhages or any siderosis were excluded, which the document states may have contributed to the observed ARIA-H incidence.
- **treatment-group assignment for pooled analysis** — Treatment groups in the pooled analysis are based on target dose in the randomized regimen, and the high-dose carrier target dose was changed mid-study from 6 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg in protocol version 4, so the 6-mg/kg group is a remainder group rather than a cleanly randomized comparator.

### Limitations stated by the document

- Potential limitations of this work include the early termination of the trials (35% of participants in the EMERGE trial and 30% in the ENGAGE trial withdrew owing to early study termination), which led to fewer patients completing a full course of dosing and could potentially impact the incidence of adverse events.
- Additionally, the global phase 3 study populations lacked racial and ethnic diversity; therefore, very limited data about the frequency and severity of ARIA in diverse populations are available.
- Data are not available for initiation of treatment at other stages of disease (ie, preclinical) or later (ie, moderate to severe dementia) stages of AD, and initiation of aducanumab is not indicated in those populations.
- Important to the interpretation of ARIA-H incidence in this study is the fact that patients with preexisting microhemorrhages (more than 4) or areas of superficial siderosis were excluded from the phase 3 trials, which may have contributed to the overall observed incidence of ARIA-H.
- More real-world data on the treatment and management of ARIA, outside of the clinical trial protocols, are required to assess which medications may be effective in treating symptomatic patients with ARIA.

### Where authoritative sources disagree

*Stored as structured evidence with each position attributed to its source, not resolved into a single verdict.*

- **Was the futility analysis that terminated EMERGE and ENGAGE valid?** — The document states that after termination it was determined that key assumptions underlying the futility decision were not valid, and that final data were collected per protocol and analyzed per the prespecified analysis plan. *(the authors of this report (Salloway et al, JAMA Neurology, sponsor-affiliated analysis of EMERGE and ENGAGE))*
- **Is it acceptable to continue dosing through radiographically mild, asymptomatic ARIA-E?** — The document concludes continued dosing for asymptomatic and radiographically limited ARIA-E is feasible, because most such events did not increase in size or involve multiple brain locations and patients typically remained asymptomatic. *(the authors of this report (Salloway et al, JAMA Neurology))*
- **Which factors increase the risk of ARIA in aducanumab-treated patients?** — The document reports that only pretreatment brain microhemorrhages and ApoE ε4 carrier status were associated with increased incidence of ARIA-E, and that these same factors did not confer increased risk of ARIA-H; age, sex, AD stage and antithrombotic use showed no association with ARIA-E. *(the authors of this report (Salloway et al, JAMA Neurology))*
