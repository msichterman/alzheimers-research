**Source**: [JAMA Neurology, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2021.4161) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34807243)
**Authors**: Salloway et al.

## Takeaway

Aducanumab causes a high rate of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), affecting 41.3% of patients on the highest dose. While most cases are asymptomatic and resolve radiographically, the high incidence underscores a significant physical risk that ultimately outweighed the drug's disputed clinical benefits.

## Key findings

- 41.3% of patients receiving 10 mg/kg aducanumab experienced ARIA, most commonly presenting as brain swelling or ARIA-E (35.2%) (paper, Results).
- ApoE ε4 carriers faced a much higher risk of ARIA-E (43.0%) compared to noncarriers (20.3%) at the highest dose (paper, Table 1).
- Among patients with ARIA-E, 26.0% experienced clinical symptoms such as headache, confusion, dizziness, or nausea (paper, Results).
- Most ARIA-E events (98.2%) resolved radiographically, with 82.8% resolving within 16 weeks of detection (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

This is a secondary safety analysis of two double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 randomized clinical trials (EMERGE and ENGAGE). The integrated dataset included 3,285 adults aged 50 to 85 with early Alzheimer's disease over a 76-week placebo-controlled period.

## Trial evidence

This paper reports secondary safety data from the [EMERGE](/trials/emerge-nct02484547) and [ENGAGE](/trials/engage-nct02477800) phase 3 trials. Both trials were terminated early for futility, but the FDA controversially granted accelerated approval based on a post-hoc analysis of EMERGE. Because ENGAGE completely failed to show efficacy, this high ARIA rate became the focal point of the drug's unfavorable risk-benefit profile, culminating in Biogen abandoning the aducanumab program entirely in 2024.

## Public sentiment

Public and professional perception of aducanumab (Aduhelm) is overwhelmingly negative. While early marketing offered desperate hope, patients and caregivers quickly grew disillusioned by high costs and the severe physical risks of ARIA. The scientific community widely views the drug's FDA approval as a regulatory failure driven by statistical manipulation, noting that simply clearing amyloid plaques did not translate to a meaningful cognitive rescue.

## Caveats

- Nearly half of the participants did not complete their full dosing course because the sponsor halted the trials early, limiting the reliability of the long-term safety data.
- The incidence of ARIA in broader, real-world populations remains unclear because these global trials lacked racial and ethnic diversity.
- The authors analyze a highly contested dataset and treat the trials' early termination as a statistical mistake, minimizing the fact that the drug's clinical efficacy was heavily disputed and ultimately rejected by the market.
