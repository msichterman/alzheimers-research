**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2025](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03186989)
**Authors**: Ionis Pharmaceuticals, Inc.

## Takeaway

This registry record provides the raw safety and pharmacokinetic data for IONIS-MAPTRx (diranersen) in mild Alzheimer's disease. The drug was generally well tolerated, with procedural pain and post-lumbar puncture syndrome as the most common adverse events, establishing the foundation for further clinical testing.

## Key findings

- Maximum observed drug concentrations (Cmax) and other pharmacokinetic measures demonstrated a dose-dependent profile across the 10 mg to 115 mg cohorts (source document).
- Adverse events were predominantly mild to moderate and frequently related to the lumbar puncture administration procedure (source document).

## Methods in brief

This Phase 1 interventional study (NCT03186989) enrolled 46 participants aged 50-74 with mild Alzheimer's disease. It featured a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multiple ascending dose period (Part 1) followed by an open-label extension (Part 2), administering doses from 10 mg to 115 mg intrathecally over 37 to 64 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This document contains the raw registry data for the Phase 1b [IONIS-MAPTRx trial](/trials/maptrx-nct03186989). While this record lacks statistical conclusions, peer-reviewed analyses of this cohort were published in *Nature Medicine* (2023). The program subsequently advanced to the Phase 2 [CELIA trial](/trials/celia-nct05399888), which missed its primary clinical endpoint in early 2026 despite showing robust tau reduction.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of diranersen (BIIB080) is mixed. Patient communities historically viewed it as a major hope for validating the tau hypothesis. However, after the Phase 2 CELIA trial missed its primary clinical endpoint in 2026, retail investors and biotech observers became increasingly cautious, balancing the drug's success in lowering biomarker levels against its clinical shortfall and the burden of regular spinal injections.

## Caveats

- This document is a raw clinical trial registry record, lacking statistical analyses or peer-reviewed narrative conclusions.
- Cohort sizes were very small (4 to 13 participants per group), which limits broad generalizability of the specific adverse event rates.
