**Source**: [J Periodontol, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/JPER.20-0104) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32441775/)
**Authors**: Mark I. Ryder — UCSF Division of Periodontology; paid Clinical Advisory Board member/consultant for Cortexyme, Inc., maker of COR388

## Takeaway

This narrative review argues P. gingivalis and its gingipain proteases contribute to Alzheimer's pathology, and previewed early COR388 trial data as supportive. The mechanistic evidence (brain/CSF detection, mouse neuropathology) is moderate but comes almost entirely from one company-funded research group. The only adequately powered human test of the hypothesis — Cortexyme's Phase 2/3 GAIN trial — later failed both co-primary endpoints and the program was discontinued after a liver-toxicity hold. The review's author is a paid Cortexyme advisor, a conflict embedded in its central evidence.

## Key findings

- P. gingivalis DNA and gingipains detected in autopsy brain and CSF of AD patients, more abundant than in controls and correlated with tau/ubiquitin pathology (paper, Section 3, Fig. 2; Dominy et al. 2019)
- Oral P. gingivalis infection produced AD-like neuropathology — plaques, tau tangles, neurodegeneration — across four independent mouse studies (paper, Section 3)
- Gingipain inhibitors reduced brain bacterial burden and neurotoxicity in mice, outperforming antibiotics and a beta-amyloid-production inhibitor (paper, Section 4)
- Phase 1a/b trial ([NCT03418688](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03418688), n=33) found COR388 well tolerated over 28 days and reduced CSF ApoE fragmentation in a 9-subject AD cohort — a biomarker result from a conference abstract, not a peer-reviewed clinical endpoint (paper, Section 4)

## Methods in brief

Narrative/invited review presented at a 2019 UNC symposium; not a systematic review, and no independent original data. Synthesizes prior human observational association studies, autopsy/CSF pathogen-detection studies, four mouse infection models, and one completed Phase 1a/b human trial (n=33). No search strategy or study-selection criteria are described.

## Trial evidence

The review previewed the pivotal Phase 2/3 GAIN trial ([NCT03823404](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03823404), n=643) as 'now underway.' It has since completed: topline results (Oct 2021) missed both co-primary endpoints (ADAS-Cog11, ADCS-ADL) at 48 weeks. A subgroup positive for oral P. gingivalis DNA (described by the sponsor as pre-specified) showed a slowed-decline signal, but this was not a confirmed primary result. Dose-dependent liver enzyme elevations triggered an FDA clinical hold in January 2022, and Cortexyme discontinued the program in August 2022, rebranding as Quince Therapeutics. See the [GAIN trial page](/trials/cor388-nct03823404) for detail.

## Public sentiment

Public reaction splits sharply by audience: biotech investors and media treat the GAIN failure, stock crash, and FDA hold as a cautionary tale, while dental and consumer-health coverage through 2025 continues to promote the P. gingivalis-Alzheimer's link and oral-hygiene prevention advice, largely without mentioning that COR388 already failed and was shelved ([STAT News, 2021](https://www.statnews.com/2021/10/26/cortexyme-bacteria-fighting-alzheimers-drug-fails-primary-goal/); [University Hospitals, 2025](https://www.uhhospitals.org/blog/articles/2025/07/gum-disease-is-linked-to-alzheimers-and-other-dementias)).

## Caveats

- Author Mark Ryder is a paid Cortexyme clinical advisor; the mechanistic and therapeutic sections rely almost entirely on Cortexyme-funded data (Dominy et al. 2019) and an unpublished conference abstract
- No systematic review methodology — no search strategy or study-selection criteria — and no negative or null P. gingivalis/AD findings are discussed
- CSF ApoE fragmentation reduction is a pharmacodynamic biomarker in 9 subjects, not a validated clinical or cognitive endpoint
- The pivotal human efficacy test (GAIN) was negative on both co-primary endpoints and the program was later halted for liver toxicity — outcomes unknown at time of writing
