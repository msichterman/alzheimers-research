## Takeaway

Lecanemab received accelerated FDA approval based on robust, dose-dependent brain amyloid plaque clearance in the Phase 2b Study 201. The regulatory decision accepted plaque reduction as a surrogate for clinical benefit, overriding the trial's failure to meet its prespecified Bayesian success threshold on the primary cognitive endpoint.

## Key findings

- Lecanemab (10 mg/kg biweekly) significantly reduced brain amyloid plaque by Week 79 compared to placebo (-0.31 SUVR; -73.5 Centiloids, p&lt;0.001) (Section 6).
- The primary clinical endpoint (ADCOMS at Week 53) achieved only a 64% probability of 25% superiority, missing the 80% prespecified Bayesian threshold for success (Section 6).
- ARIA-E occurred in 10% of treated patients versus 1% on placebo, with incidence heavily influenced by ApoE ε4 carrier status (Section 7, Table 5).
- ApoE ε4 homozygotes faced a 50% risk of ARIA-E (5 of 10 patients) compared to 5% in heterozygotes and 8% in non-carriers, though group sizes were exceptionally small (Section 7).

## Methods in brief

Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2b trial (Study 201) enrolling 856 patients aged 50-90 with MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia. The placebo-controlled treatment period lasted 18 months (78 weeks), utilizing adaptive Bayesian randomization to allocate patients to placebo or one of five lecanemab dose regimens.

## Trial evidence

This FDA review relies exclusively on the Phase 2b Study 201 ([NCT01767311](/trials/lecanemab-nct01767311)), which failed its primary clinical endpoint but met its surrogate biomarker endpoint. Subsequent full traditional approval was granted in July 2023 following the readout of the confirmatory Phase 3 Clarity AD trial (NCT03887455), which demonstrated statistically significant clinical slowing.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is characterized by a mix of desperation for disease-modifying therapies and anxiety over ARIA side effects and logistical burdens. While the biotech community views the drug as a clear scientific validation of the amyloid hypothesis, consumer-facing media often overstates the clinical benefits, mistakenly characterizing the drug as reversing memory loss rather than modestly slowing decline.

## Caveats

- Safety estimates for ApoE ε4 carriers at the approved dose are inherently imprecise; early trial protocol amendments halted their randomization to the 10 mg/kg arm after ARIA risks became apparent.
- The FDA's decision to accept amyloid clearance as a surrogate endpoint was heavily influenced by prior precedent set by aducanumab, overriding internal biostatistician concerns over the exploratory nature of the clinical data.
- The approved label delegates the decision of when to stop treatment entirely to prescriber clinical judgment, acknowledging that the disease exists on a continuous spectrum without discrete boundaries.

### Bias & spin flags

- Study 201 failed to achieve its prespecified Bayesian success criterion (80% probability) on the primary clinical endpoint (ADCOMS at Week 53), achieving only 64%.
- The statistical analysis plan for Study 201 did not control for type I error across interim analyses, prompting the FDA biostatistics reviewer to classify the efficacy results as strictly exploratory.
