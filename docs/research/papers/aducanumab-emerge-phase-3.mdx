**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2021](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02484547)
**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

Despite being terminated early for futility, post-hoc analysis of the EMERGE trial claimed high-dose aducanumab significantly slowed clinical decline. This result was highly controversial because an identically designed twin trial (ENGAGE) completely failed to replicate it, and the drug caused high rates of brain swelling and bleeding.

## Key findings

- High-dose aducanumab slowed clinical decline on the CDR-SB scale compared to placebo (difference -0.39, p=0.0120) (Source document, Primary outcomes).
- The high-dose group showed statistically significant improvements on secondary cognitive and functional endpoints including the MMSE, ADAS-Cog 13, and ADCS-ADL-MCI (Source document, Secondary outcomes).
- ARIA-E (edema/effusion) occurred in 33.5% of high-dose patients, compared to 2.2% in the placebo group (Source document, Adverse events).

## Methods in brief

A Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. It evaluated monthly intravenous aducanumab (low and high doses) versus placebo in 1,643 adults aged 50-85 with early Alzheimer's disease and positive amyloid PET scans over 78 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This registry record represents the EMERGE trial ([NCT02484547](/trials/aducanumab-emerge-nct02484547)), the highly debated cornerstone of aducanumab's accelerated FDA approval. The evidence is severely compromised by the complete failure of its twin trial, ENGAGE ([NCT02477800](/trials/aducanumab-engage-nct02477800)), to show any clinical efficacy. Citing lack of robust evidence and commercial failure, Biogen discontinued the aducanumab program in 2024.

## Public sentiment

Public and scientific perception of aducanumab is overwhelmingly negative, functioning today as a cautionary tale of regulatory failure. While FDA approval initially generated hope among patients, subsequent revelations of exorbitant pricing, severe ARIA risks, and the FDA's decision to bypass its own advisory committee fostered deep medical mistrust.

## Caveats

- The official trial status is 'terminated for futility'; the positive efficacy endpoints reflect a highly selected post-hoc analysis rather than the planned primary analysis.
- The findings are un-replicated anomalies, heavily contradicted by the identically designed ENGAGE trial.
- This document is a structured registry data dump, entirely lacking the qualitative context, discussion of limitations, or peer-review typical of a publication.
