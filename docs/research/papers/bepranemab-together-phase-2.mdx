**Authors**: UCB Biopharma SRL

## Takeaway

The Phase 2 TOGETHER trial tested the anti-tau monoclonal antibody bepranemab in 466 patients with early Alzheimer's disease. While full peer-reviewed results are not yet in the registry, conference abstracts and media reports indicate the drug successfully slowed tau accumulation but missed its primary clinical efficacy endpoint (CDR-SB) in the overall cohort. This led to the termination of a major pharmaceutical partnership, leaving only a narrow window of optimism for specific subgroups.

## Key findings

- Enrolled 466 adults aged 50 to 80 with prodromal to mild AD and confirmed amyloid accumulation (source document).
- Slowed tau accumulation by 33-58% versus placebo on tau-PET, according to December 2025 conference abstracts (trials researcher).
- Failed to demonstrate broad clinical efficacy on the primary CDR-SB endpoint in the overall cohort (sentiment and trials researchers).
- Post-hoc exposure-response modeling suggested potential clinical benefit in a subpopulation of low-tau, APOE4 non-carriers (trials researcher).

## Methods in brief

A Phase 2, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled trial (NCT04867616) that randomized 466 participants to receive one of two doses of bepranemab or placebo. The double-blind treatment period lasted 80 weeks, followed by an open-label extension. The primary endpoint was the change in Clinical Dementia Rating Scale Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) score at Week 80.

## Trial evidence

This document is the primary registry record for the pivotal [TOGETHER (NCT04867616)](/trials/bepranemab-nct04867616) trial. Although the registry itself contains no results, external abstracts indicate the trial demonstrated strong target engagement (lowering tau) but failed to show broad clinical efficacy. This aligns bepranemab with a growing list of anti-tau therapies that clear tangles without immediately translating to cognitive stabilization.

## Public sentiment

Public and investor sentiment is largely negative or highly skeptical following the trial's failure to meet its broad clinical endpoints. Biotech media focused heavily on partner Roche terminating its $2 billion licensing deal in late 2024. Mainstream patient communities remain largely unaware of the drug, though some scientific commentators maintain cautious optimism about the drug's clear ability to clear tau and its potential in low-tau subgroups.

## Caveats

- The source document is a trial registry record without posted results; all clinical outcome data currently relies on conference abstracts and external media reports.
- The reported clinical benefits are derived from post-hoc modeling of a specific subpopulation (low-tau, APOE4 non-carriers), which carries a high risk of statistical noise and requires prospective validation.
