## Takeaway

Fourteen modifiable risk factors—including high LDL cholesterol and vision loss, newly added in 2024—account for an estimated 45% of worldwide dementia cases. Additionally, non-pharmacological interventions that are tailored and psychosocial (like TAP and WHELD) improve quality of life and reduce behavioral symptoms, whereas purely physical exercise programs generally do not benefit cognition in patients who already have dementia.

## Key findings

- 14 modifiable risk factors account for a weighted Population Attributable Fraction (PAF) of 45.3% of global dementia cases (Table 1).
- Hearing loss (7.0%) and high LDL cholesterol (6.9%) are the highest midlife contributors to dementia risk (Table 1).
- Tailored activity and well-being programs (WHELD, TAP) successfully improve quality of life and reduce neuropsychiatric symptoms like agitation (Table 2).
- Purely physical exercise interventions (DAPA, Harwood, Sanders) do not improve cognition, mobility, or activities of daily living in people who already have dementia (Table 2).

## Methods in brief

Systematic review and epidemiological modeling. Prevalence data for the PAF model was derived from global populations, though high LDL cholesterol prevalence (76.5%) was extrapolated entirely from 37,000 participants in the Norwegian HUNT study. The report also reviewed randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of non-pharmacological activity programs for people with dementia and their carers.

## Trial evidence

The Lancet report aligns directly with published trial data for non-pharmacological interventions. RCTs of personalized, multidomain psychosocial programs ([WHELD](/trials/wheld-nct01855152), [TAP](/trials/tap-nct00259467)) demonstrated significant reductions in behavioral symptoms and improved quality of life. In contrast, rigorous RCTs focused exclusively on aerobic or strength training ([DAPA](/trials/dapa-isrctn10416500), PrAISED, Sanders) consistently failed to show cognitive or functional benefits for patients who already have dementia.

## Public sentiment

Public and media sentiment is overwhelmingly hopeful, focusing heavily on the empowering message that nearly half of dementia cases are linked to actionable lifestyle choices. However, popular coverage frequently misinterprets the 45% Population Attributable Fraction (a theoretical societal metric) as an individual guarantee of prevention. Furthermore, wellness communities often mistakenly promote standalone physical exercise as a proven cognitive treatment for existing dementia patients, contrary to the report's actual findings.

## Caveats

- The 45% figure is a Population Attributable Fraction (PAF), meaning it assumes the complete, theoretical elimination of these risks across the entire population, not a 45% reduced risk for an individual.
- Prevalence data for high LDL cholesterol is extrapolated from a specific regional cohort (the Norwegian HUNT study) rather than a globally representative sample.
- While physical inactivity is a risk factor for *developing* dementia (2% PAF), the evidence explicitly does not support exercise alone as an effective intervention for improving cognition once dementia has begun.
