## Takeaway

By running GWAS against specific brain pathologies rather than clinical diagnoses, researchers identified novel genetic drivers for vascular dementia contributors (*COL4A1*, *LZTS1*, *APOC2*). The study also clarified that some known dementia risk genes (like *GRN* and *TMEM106B*) primarily drive non-Alzheimer's pathologies such as hippocampal sclerosis and LATE, rather than classic amyloid or tau.

## Key findings

- Identified three novel risk loci: *LZTS1* for arteriolosclerosis, *COL4A1* for atherosclerosis, and *APOC2* for cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) (paper, Table 2).
- The *APOC2* locus association with CAA remained highly significant even after adjusting for known *APOE* alleles, marking an independent genetic risk factor in the APOE region (paper, Results).
- Known dementia loci *TMEM106B* and *GRN* were significantly associated with non-AD pathologies (hippocampal sclerosis and LATE-NC) but not with classic amyloid plaques or neurofibrillary tangles (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Genome-wide association study (GWAS) meta-analysis of 7,804 deceased participants from the NACC, ROSMAP, and ACT cohorts with available autopsy and genotype data.

## Trial evidence

The novel loci (*APOC2*, *COL4A1*, *LZTS1*) are foundational discoveries with no active clinical trials targeting them for dementia. While the study reinforces the role of *GRN* in non-AD pathologies, clinical translation of the *GRN* pathway recently faced a major setback when the Phase 3 [INFRONT-3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04374136) trial of latozinemab for FTD-GRN was terminated for lack of efficacy.

## Public sentiment

Public and investor sentiment is quiet, as the findings represent early-stage foundational genetics rather than actionable therapeutic breakthroughs. Media coverage was largely limited to standard institutional press releases.

## Caveats

- The study population was restricted to participants of European descent, limiting the generalizability of these genetic associations to other ancestries.
- Three examined pathologies (gross infarcts, microinfarcts, and Lewy bodies) yielded no genome-wide significant loci, likely due to sample size constraints or heterogeneity in grading across research centers.
