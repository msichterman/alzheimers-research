**Source**: [Alzheimer's & Dementia : Translational Research & Clinical Interventions, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/trc2.12158) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33778150)
**Authors**: Potter et al. — University of Colorado

## Takeaway

Sargramostim (GM-CSF) proved safe over a 3-week treatment period and successfully activated the innate immune system. However, claims of cognitive improvement are highly questionable due to a severe baseline imbalance between groups, a very small sample size (N=40), and worsening scores on the standard ADAS-Cog13 assessment.

## Key findings

- Sargramostim met its primary safety endpoint over 3 weeks of treatment, with no drug-related serious adverse events or ARIA (paper, RESULTS).
- Treatment significantly increased innate immune cells, including monocytes, lymphocytes, and neutrophils (paper, Figure 1).
- MMSE scores improved by 1.45 units from baseline at end-of-treatment, but the sargramostim group started with significantly worse baseline scores (17.10) than placebo (20.75) (paper, Table 1).
- Plasma biomarkers showed a 10% increase in Aβ40 and a 24% reduction in total tau compared to placebo (paper, Figure 4).
- Standard cognitive measures (ADAS-Cog13) showed no benefit at the end of treatment and statistically worsened 45 days later (paper, Figure 3).

## Methods in brief

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial (N=40 analyzed) in adults with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease (MMSE 10-26). Participants received subcutaneous sargramostim or placebo 5 days/week for 3 weeks, with follow-ups at 45 and 90 days. Registered as NCT01409915.

## Trial evidence

This paper reports results from a single, underpowered Phase 2 trial ([NCT01409915](/trials/sargramostim-nct01409915)). While the document reports no drug-related serious adverse events, the registry notes one myocardial infarction in the treatment group (likely deemed unrelated). The cognitive findings are heavily compromised by the baseline imbalances confirmed in the registry. An ongoing 24-week Phase 2 trial ([NCT04902703](/trials/sargramostim-nct04902703)) is attempting to replicate these preliminary results, following the termination of an earlier Sanofi-sponsored MCI trial ([NCT02667496](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02667496)).

## Public sentiment

Public perception is sharply divided. While university press releases and general media celebrated the drug as a repurposed cancer treatment that improves memory, the scientific community (e.g., Alzforum) remains highly skeptical. Researchers point to the tiny sample size, the short duration, the baseline cognitive imbalances, and the worsening of standard cognitive endpoints at follow-up. A minor fringe community has also shown unsafe curiosity about off-label use for cognitive enhancement.

## Caveats

- A severe baseline imbalance (placebo MMSE 20.75 vs sargramostim 17.10) confounds all cognitive efficacy claims; adjusting for this baseline erased the statistical significance of the end-of-treatment MMSE benefit.
- The 3-week treatment period is exceptionally short for an Alzheimer's disease trial, making any lasting cognitive or biomarker claims highly speculative.
- Promotional materials selectively emphasize the positive MMSE results while omitting the failure and subsequent significant worsening of the gold-standard ADAS-Cog13 metric.

### Bias & spin flags

- Baseline imbalance in cognitive scores: The placebo group started with noticeably higher baseline MMSE scores (20.75 vs 17.10 in the treatment group).
- Analytical flexibility to achieve significance: The authors state that when adjusting for the significant baseline MMSE differences as covariates, the primary cognitive treatment effect at EOT was 'only marginally statistically non-significant' (P = 0.0808), prompting them to use an unadjusted cell means model instead.
- Selective emphasis on positive endpoints: The abstract highlights the MMSE improvements but omits that the ADAS-Cog13 (a standard AD clinical endpoint) showed no significant benefit at the end of treatment and statistically significant worsening (P = 0.0009) at the 45-day follow-up.
