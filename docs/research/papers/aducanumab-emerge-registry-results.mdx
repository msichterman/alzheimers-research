**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2021](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02484547)
**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

The Phase 3 EMERGE trial of aducanumab was terminated early for futility, but a post-hoc analysis of the final dataset showed statistically significant clinical slowing of decline in the high-dose group. However, these results are severely confounded by the premature halt and completely contradicted by the failure of its identical twin trial, ENGAGE.

## Key findings

- High-dose aducanumab significantly slowed clinical decline on the primary CDR-SB endpoint compared to placebo (difference -0.39, p=0.0120) (registry data, PRIMARY outcome).
- Secondary cognitive and functional endpoints (MMSE, ADAS-Cog 13, ADCS-ADL-MCI) nominally favored the high-dose group (registry data, SECONDARY outcomes).
- ARIA-E (edema) occurred in 33.5% (183/547) of high-dose participants versus 2.2% (12/547) on placebo (registry data, Other adverse events).
- ARIA-H (microhemorrhages) occurred in 19.4% (106/547) of high-dose participants versus 6.8% (37/547) on placebo (registry data, Other adverse events).

## Methods in brief

Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 study evaluating 1,643 adults aged 50-85 with mild cognitive impairment or mild Alzheimer's disease and positive amyloid PET scans. The primary outcome timeframe was 78 weeks, but the study was terminated early for futility (NCT02484547).

## Trial evidence

EMERGE served as one of two pivotal Phase 3 trials for aducanumab. Although this registry record shows efficacy for the high-dose group, the identical twin [ENGAGE](/trials/engage-nct02477800) trial simultaneously failed to show clinical benefit. Both trials were prematurely terminated for futility, heavily compromising the statistical validity of EMERGE's positive findings. Due to these irresolvable discrepancies, safety concerns, and commercial failure, Biogen eventually terminated the post-approval [ENVISION](/trials/envision-nct05310071) trial and permanently discontinued the aducanumab program.

## Public sentiment

Public and scientific sentiment is overwhelmingly skeptical. While some desperate patients initially hoped for a breakthrough, discourse quickly fixated on the drug's prohibitive cost, severe ARIA risks, and the highly criticized FDA decision to approve the drug based on amyloid clearance rather than definitive clinical benefit.

## Caveats

- The trial was terminated early for futility, violating the prespecified statistical framework and heavily confounding the final post-hoc dataset.
- The positive efficacy signal exists in isolation; the identical Phase 3 ENGAGE trial failed to demonstrate clinical benefit.
- As a raw registry extract, the source lacks peer-reviewed methodology or contextualized clinical discussion.
