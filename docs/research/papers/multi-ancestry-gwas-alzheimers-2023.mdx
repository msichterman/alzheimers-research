**Source**: [Molecular Psychiatry, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-023-02089-w) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37198259)
**Authors**: Julie Lake et al. — Data Tecnica International LLC / NIH

## Takeaway

This meta-analysis highlights the concrete value of diversifying genetic datasets beyond European populations. By including East Asian, African American, and Caribbean Hispanic cohorts, the study successfully pinpointed single likely causal variants at nine known Alzheimer's risk loci (including *GRN*) and demonstrated that multi-ancestry polygenic risk scores are significantly better at predicting disease in admixed populations.

## Key findings

- Improved fine-mapping resolution at nine known risk loci to a 99% credible set of 1–2 variants, including *GRN*, *RHOH*, and *CTSB* (paper, Table 2).
- Multi-ancestry polygenic risk scores achieved a 79% predictive AUC in an admixed Colombian cohort, outperforming the 75% AUC of European-only scores (paper, Fig. 4).
- Nominated two putative new risk loci on chromosome 3 (*TRANK1* and *VWA5B2*), though the statistical signals were sub-significant in the primary model.

## Methods in brief

Multi-ancestry genome-wide association study (GWAS) meta-analysis evaluating 644,188 individuals (54,233 clinical AD cases, 46,828 proxy-AD cases, and 543,127 controls). Cohorts incorporated European, East Asian, African American, and Caribbean Hispanic ancestries.

## Trial evidence

This study provides genetic validation for established clinical targets rather than evaluating interventions. Notably, it successfully fine-mapped the *GRN* locus to a single likely causal variant. *GRN* encodes progranulin, the target of several active clinical programs including [PROGRESS-AD](/trials/progress-ad-nct06079190), a Phase 2 trial of the monoclonal antibody AL101/GSK4527226 in early Alzheimer's.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is exceptionally quiet outside of academic circles. Specialized genetic and bioinformatics communities focus heavily on the study's methodological implications—specifically the urgent need for non-European genomic data to address Eurocentric bias in risk prediction—rather than focusing on the weakly supported novel risk loci.

## Caveats

- The practical utility of the multi-ancestry polygenic risk scores was tested solely in an admixed Colombian cohort; broad validation across other heavily admixed global populations is needed to confirm true generalizability.
- The discovery of the two 'novel' loci is statistically fragile; the field requires rigorous replication before treating them as definitive Alzheimer's risk factors.
- Because roughly 85% of the underlying genomic data remains European, the mathematical ability to fine-map variants that are unique to African or Asian ancestries is still fundamentally restricted.

### Bias & spin flags

- The two highlighted 'novel' risk loci (TRANK1 and VWA5B2) were sub-significant in the study's primary multi-ancestry model (MR-MEGA) and failed to replicate in the independent East Asian cohort (VWA5B2 actually showed an opposite effect direction).
