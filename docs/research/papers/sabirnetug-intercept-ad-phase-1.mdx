**Source**: [The Journal of Prevention of Alzheimer's Disease, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tjpad.2024.100005) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39800458/)
**Authors**: Acumen Pharmaceuticals (Sponsor)

## Takeaway

In this Phase 1 safety study, the Aβ oligomer-targeting antibody sabirnetug demonstrated dose-dependent target engagement in the cerebrospinal fluid. The drug was generally well-tolerated with a 10.4% ARIA-E rate, but it also produced unexpected reductions in amyloid plaque despite being designed to selectively spare plaques.

## Key findings

- ARIA-E occurred in 10.4% (5 of 48) of treated participants and was mostly asymptomatic (paper, Results).
- Zero cases of ARIA-E were observed in APOE ε4 homozygotes, though this subgroup contained only 6 participants (paper, Results).
- High doses produced an approximate 20–25% reduction in amyloid plaque load on PET scans over three months, an unexpected finding for an oligomer-selective antibody (paper, Results).
- Target engagement (sabirnetug-AβO complex) in CSF approached maximal response at 60 mg/kg every four weeks and 25 mg/kg every two weeks (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, ascending-dose Phase 1 trial ([NCT04931459](/trials/sabirnetug-nct04931459)) in 65 adults (aged 55–90) with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to AD. Participants received either a single dose with 20-week follow-up or three doses over 8–12 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This paper establishes the initial clinical evidence for sabirnetug via the Phase 1 [INTERCEPT-AD](/trials/sabirnetug-nct04931459) trial. While the trial verified safety and target engagement, it was not powered to test clinical efficacy, and PET/cognitive measures were not pre-specified endpoints in the trial registry. Disease-modifying efficacy is currently being evaluated in the fully powered Phase 2 [ALTITUDE-AD](/trials/sabirnetug-nct06335173) trial, expected to read out in late 2026.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is largely hopeful among patient communities and highly enthusiastic among retail investors, who view the drug as a 'next-generation' targeted therapy. However, this hype has generated online misinformation—specifically overstating the drug's safety in APOE ε4 homozygotes based on a six-person sample, and incorrectly claiming it entirely ignores amyloid plaques despite Phase 1 PET data showing significant plaque reduction.

## Caveats

- The study was sponsored, designed, and analyzed entirely by Acumen Pharmaceuticals employees, directly contradicting the paper's formal 'None' declaration for competing interests.
- Amyloid plaque reduction and cognitive outcomes are emphasized in the text but were not pre-specified as primary or secondary endpoints in the official trial registry.
- The claim that APOE ε4 homozygotes are protected from ARIA-E relies on a subsample of only six participants, which is statistically insufficient for broad safety generalizations.
