## Takeaway

This document outlines the protocol for Clarity AD, the pivotal Phase 3 trial that led to lecanemab's FDA approval. Although the registry itself is marked 'active' with no results posted due to an ongoing open-label extension, the core 18-month study successfully concluded and demonstrated a 27% slowing of clinical decline.

## Key findings

- The core efficacy study ran for 18 months, with a primary endpoint measuring the change in CDR-SB score (source document).
- Secondary endpoints tracked amyloid PET changes, ADAS-cog14, ADCOMS, and ADCS MCI-ADL (source document).
- The ongoing extension phases are assessing long-term safety, maintenance, and subcutaneous (SC) injection formulations (source document).

## Methods in brief

A Phase 3, randomized, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolling 1,906 adults (aged 50-90) with mild cognitive impairment or mild AD. Participants were required to have positive brain amyloid biomarkers and an MMSE score between 22 and 30. The core study ran for 18 months, followed by long-term extension phases scheduled through 2029 (NCT03887455).

## Trial evidence

This is the primary registry record for Clarity AD ([NCT03887455](/trials/lecanemab-clarity-ad-nct03887455)), the definitive trial that secured traditional FDA approval for lecanemab. It builds upon the dose-dependent amyloid clearance established in the earlier Phase 2b study ([Study 201](/research/papers/lecanemab-phase-2b)). While the registry lacks results, the 18-month Phase 3 readout published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in January 2023 confirmed the efficacy signal.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is deeply mixed. While many patients and caregivers view the drug as a historic milestone, there is widespread frustration regarding its grueling biweekly infusion schedule, flu-like side effects, and the anxiety of mandatory MRI monitoring for ARIA (brain bleeding or swelling). Because the drug only slows decline rather than restoring memory, patients often report distress over a lack of visible improvement. Meanwhile, investors are enthusiastic about the potential for subcutaneous formulations, and the scientific community balances the breakthrough validation of the amyloid hypothesis against the significant safety risks.

## Caveats

- Because this document is only the registry protocol, it lacks the actual peer-reviewed efficacy and safety data published separately in 2023.
- The registry's 'active' status and 'no results' flag reflect the ongoing 2029 extension phase; they misleadingly suggest the primary efficacy question is still unanswered.
