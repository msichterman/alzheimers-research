## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: not_met.** The primary outcome — change from baseline to week 104 in CDR-SB — showed no benefit of verubecestat over placebo (1.65 in the 12-mg group and 2.02 in the 40-mg group vs. 1.58 in the placebo group, P = 0.67 and P = 0.01 respectively), with the 40-mg comparison significantly favouring placebo, and the trial was terminated for futility.
> verubecestat showed no benefit with respect to the primary clinical outcome (a change in the CDR-SB score from baseline to week 104), as compared with placebo
**Multiplicity handling:** Bonferroni approach splitting the overall alpha between the two dose levels of verubecestat, in conjunction with a hierarchical sequential testing approach of outcomes; once superiority was not shown for the primary CDR-SB outcome, all subsequent outcomes were assumed not to have differed significantly and are reported as point estimates with 97.51% confidence intervals (95% for exploratory outcomes) not adjusted for multiple comparisons

### Risk of bias

- **sponsor analysis** — The sponsor designed the trial and analysed the data; the first manuscript draft was written with a sponsor-employed medical writer, and confidentiality agreements were in place between sponsor and authors.
- **attrition** — Fewer than half of enrolled patients completed part 1, and completion numbers fell well short of the planned 338 per group because of the early stop.
- **early termination for futility** — The trial was stopped about 12 months before scheduled completion of part 1 on the recommendation of the data and safety monitoring committee, reducing the information available for all outcomes.
- **multiplicity and selective inference** — After the primary outcome failed, the hierarchical gatekeeping plan precluded formal inference; all subsequent estimates are reported with unadjusted confidence intervals, which the paper itself says limits inference.
- **incomplete safety ascertainment** — MRI surveillance for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities was stopped part-way through the trial, so the absence of ARIA is based on incomplete imaging follow-up.
- **internal reporting inconsistency** — The abstract and the Results section assign the 234 and 231 completer counts to different groups: the abstract reads 234, 231, 239 in the order 12-mg, 40-mg, placebo, while the Results text reads 231 in the 12-mg group and 234 in the 40-mg group.

### Limitations stated by the document

- The comparisons beyond the primary outcome are exploratory with unadjusted confidence intervals: "These comparisons are exploratory, and the confidence intervals are unadjusted; thus, the strength of any resulting inferences is limited."
- The trial was not powered for time-point or subgroup analyses: "Analyses at other time points and subgroup anaIyses were prespecified, but the triaI was not powered for such anaIyses."
- Early termination for futility meant far fewer completers than planned: "the number of patients who completed the trial at week 104 in each group (231 in the 12-mg group, 234 in the 40-mg group, and 239 patients in the placebo group) () was less than the 338 patients per group who had been expected to complete the trial."
- No patients completed the extension phase: "None of the patients completed the extension phase because of the early termination"
- The cerebrospinal fluid biomarker substudy was too small for its planned analyses: "Since there were few patients in the substudy of biomarkers in cerebrospinal fluid (five or six patients per group), planned analyses were not performed"
- Formal statistical inference for outcomes after the primary was precluded by the hierarchical plan: "A formal statistical analysis was not performed because of the hierarchical analysis plan and failure to show superiority of the drug in the analysis of the primary outcome"
- Earlier time-point findings are suggestive only: "suggesting but not confirming the possibility of worse performance at these earlier time points in the high-dose verubecestat group"
- MRI surveillance for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities was discontinued during the trial: "MRI was initially performed to assess for possible amyloid-related imaging abnormalities but was subsequently discontinued on the basis of feedback from the independent monitoring committee and regulatory recommendations."

### Where authoritative sources disagree

*Stored as structured evidence with each position attributed to its source, not resolved into a single verdict.*

- **Does BACE-1 inhibition with verubecestat benefit patients with prodromal Alzheimer's disease?** — No. Verubecestat did not improve clinical ratings of dementia, and some measures suggested that cognition and daily function were worse with verubecestat than with placebo. *(This document (Egan et al., the trial investigators and sponsor Merck Sharp & Dohme))*
- **Did verubecestat accelerate progression to dementia due to Alzheimer's disease?** — The hazard ratios for progression to dementia, whose confidence intervals excluded 1 and favoured placebo, suggest that verubecestat may have accelerated progression, although formal statistical analysis was not performed. *(This document (Egan et al.))*
- **Why do these results differ from the earlier verubecestat trial in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's dementia, where no overall treatment effect was seen?** — Patients here were on average 3 to 4 years earlier in the disease course and patients at an earlier stage may be more sensitive to substantial BACE-1 inhibition, perhaps because of a role of BACE-1 in normal synaptic function; effects due to BACE-2 inhibition are also possible. *(This document (Egan et al.))*
- **Did verubecestat engage its intended target?** — Yes — cortical amyloid load declined on PET and cerebrospinal fluid A β 42 and related APP metabolites fell by more than 60%, indicating the drug was acting at its intended target despite the absence of clinical benefit. *(This document (Egan et al.))*
- **Is verubecestat associated with amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, as antiamyloid immunotherapies are?** — No. As in the previous trial, verubecestat was not associated with amyloid-related imaging abnormalities of the kind reported with antiamyloid immunotherapies. *(This document (Egan et al.))*
