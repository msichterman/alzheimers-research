**Source**: [The New England journal of medicine, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812840) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30970186)
**Authors**: Egan et al. — Merck Sharp & Dohme

## Takeaway

The Phase 3 APECS trial investigated whether blocking amyloid production with the BACE-1 inhibitor verubecestat could slow cognitive decline in prodromal Alzheimer's disease. Despite effectively reducing amyloid levels in the brain, the drug offered no clinical benefit and actually worsened cognitive outcomes at higher doses, leading to early termination for futility. This provided definitive clinical evidence against BACE-1 inhibition as a viable therapeutic strategy.

## Key findings

- Patients receiving the 40-mg dose of verubecestat showed significantly greater clinical decline on the CDR-SB (2.02 point worsening) compared to placebo (1.58 point worsening) over 104 weeks (paper, RESULTS).
- The estimated rate of progression to dementia due to Alzheimer’s disease was higher in the 40-mg group (25.5 per 100 patient-years) than in the placebo group (19.3), with a hazard ratio of 1.38 (paper, RESULTS).
- Verubecestat effectively engaged its target, reducing brain amyloid load on PET scans and lowering cerebrospinal fluid Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels by more than 60% (paper, BIOMARKERS).

## Methods in brief

This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial enrolling 1,454 adults (aged 50-85) with prodromal Alzheimer's disease. Participants received either 12 mg verubecestat, 40 mg verubecestat, or placebo daily. The trial was designed to last 104 weeks but was terminated early in February 2018 for futility.

## Trial evidence

The APECS trial ([NCT01953601](/trials/verubecestat-apecs)) was the second massive Phase 3 failure for verubecestat. It followed the early termination of the EPOCH trial ([NCT01739348](/trials/verubecestat-epoch)) in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease, which similarly found no clinical benefit despite robust target engagement. Together, these two pivotal trials definitively established that verubecestat is ineffective and potentially harmful.

## Public sentiment

Public perception shifted from immense hope to intense skepticism following verubecestat's clinical failures. Early media reports had hyped the drug's ability to 'switch off' toxic amyloid production based on preliminary safety data. The realization that amyloid clearance actually worsened cognition severely damaged confidence in both BACE-1 inhibitors and the broader amyloid hypothesis.

## Caveats

- The early termination for futility severely limited the number of patients who reached the 104-week endpoint, restricting long-term safety data.
- Because the primary endpoint failed to show superiority, the statistical hierarchy broke down; the observed cognitive worsening in the 40-mg group is technically considered an exploratory finding.
- The cerebrospinal fluid biomarker substudy only enrolled five or six patients per group, preventing formal statistical analyses on those specific fluid outcomes.
