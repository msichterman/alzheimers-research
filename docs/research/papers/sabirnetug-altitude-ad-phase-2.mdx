## Takeaway

The ALTITUDE-AD trial evaluates whether sabirnetug—a monoclonal antibody designed to specifically target amyloid-beta oligomers—can slow cognitive decline. Results are not expected until late 2026, meaning clinical efficacy remains entirely unproven.

## Key findings

- No clinical results are currently available; the trial is fully enrolled and active as of mid-2026 ([NCT06335173](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06335173)).

## Methods in brief

Phase 2, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial enrolling 542 adults (aged 50-90) with mild cognitive impairment or probable AD and evidence of amyloid accumulation. Participants receive intravenous sabirnetug (35 mg/kg or 50 mg/kg) or placebo every four weeks over an 80-week period ([NCT06335173](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06335173)).

## Trial evidence

Sabirnetug's clinical evidence currently relies on the completed Phase 1 [INTERCEPT-AD (NCT04931459)](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04931459) trial, which demonstrated safety and central target engagement. Efficacy hinges on the outcome of the ongoing pivotal [ALTITUDE-AD](/trials/sabirnetug-nct06335173) study.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is sharply divided: retail investors have generated significant speculative hype equating Phase 1 biomarker data with clinical success, while patients and caregivers remain largely unaware of the drug. Scientific media maintains a measured outlook focused on the drug's mechanism.

## Caveats

- Investor and market enthusiasm currently outpaces the scientific reality, as Phase 1 data only established safety and target engagement, not clinical benefit.
