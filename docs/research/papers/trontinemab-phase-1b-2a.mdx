**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2020](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04639050)
**Authors**: Hoffmann-La Roche

## Takeaway

This registry describes the Phase 1b/2a multiple-ascending dose trial of trontinemab (RO7126209), an anti-amyloid antibody fused to a Brainshuttle module. Although the registry itself contains no results, external interim readouts have prompted Roche to accelerate the drug into multiple pivotal Phase 3 trials.

## Key findings

- The trial evaluates multiple ascending intravenous doses in 241 participants across dose-finding and expansion cohorts (registry).
- Primary endpoints focus on safety (percentage of participants with adverse events) and efficacy via change in brain amyloid load on PET scans (registry).

## Methods in brief

A Phase 1b/2a, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multiple-ascending dose study (NCT04639050) enrolling 241 adults (aged 50-85) with prodromal or mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease and positive amyloid PET scans. The study spans a dose-finding phase, an expansion phase, a dose/frequency relationship phase, and an open-label extension lasting up to 233 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This is the registry record for the foundational Phase 1b/2a Brainshuttle AD trial ([NCT04639050](/trials/brainshuttle-ad-nct04639050)). Based on highly encouraging interim biomarker and safety readouts presented at conferences, Roche has advanced the drug directly into a massive Phase 3 program (TRONTIER 1, TRONTIER 2, and PrevenTRON) before this Phase 2 trial has fully completed.

## Public sentiment

The scientific and patient communities are highly optimistic about trontinemab's blood-brain barrier-crossing mechanism. Enthusiasts anticipate that the Brainshuttle technology might clear amyloid much faster and safely bypass the vascular accumulation that causes ARIA. However, caution remains among some experts who warn against over-interpreting early surrogate biomarker data before Phase 3 clinical efficacy is proven.

## Caveats

- The registry record itself contains no efficacy or safety data; claims of rapid amyloid clearance currently rely entirely on interim conference abstracts.
- Reductions in amyloid PET load and downstream biomarkers do not automatically guarantee that trontinemab will successfully slow cognitive decline.
- Long-term safety, especially the durable ARIA risk profile at higher doses in a broad population, cannot be established until Phase 3 readouts.
