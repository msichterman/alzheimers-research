## Takeaway

The 2024 Alzheimer's Association criteria fundamentally shift the definition of Alzheimer's disease from a clinical syndrome to a biological process. The framework establishes that an abnormal 'Core 1' biomarker (amyloid PET, specific CSF ratios, or highly accurate plasma p-tau) is sufficient for diagnosis, and proposes independent scales for biological disease severity and clinical symptoms.

## Key findings

- An abnormal Core 1 biomarker is sufficient to establish a biological diagnosis of Alzheimer's, as 95% of symptomatic individuals with moderate/frequent neuritic plaques have intermediate/high neuropathologic change (paper, DIAGNOSIS).
- Highly accurate blood-based biomarkers (BBMs), particularly p-tau217, that achieve >90% accuracy against reference standards can serve as standalone diagnostic tests (paper, Anchoring Core 1 biomarkers).
- Biological severity is staged from A to D based on the sequential progression of amyloid and tau biomarkers, independent of the 6-stage clinical severity scale (paper, BIOLOGICAL DISEASE STAGING).

## Methods in brief

Workgroup consensus and criteria formulation by the Alzheimer's Association, updating the 2018 NIA-AA research framework to incorporate new evidence on blood-based biomarkers and the approval of anti-amyloid therapies.

## Trial evidence

These criteria reflect a paradigm shift validated by recent Phase 3 trials like [Clarity AD](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03887455) (lecanemab) and [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04437511) (donanemab), which demonstrated that targeting biological markers yields clinical benefit. The guidelines lean heavily on observational cohorts like [ADNI3](/trials/adni3-nct02854033) and utility studies like [IDEAS](/trials/ideas-amyloid-pet-nct02420756) to establish the sequence and clinical validity of biomarkers. Notably, while the document endorses highly accurate blood-based biomarkers as standalone tests, no standalone plasma assay has yet received full FDA clearance for this purpose.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is highly polarized. While there is broad optimism about accessible blood tests, the shift toward biological diagnosis in asymptomatic patients has sparked severe anxiety and confusion, leading some to seek unproven lifestyle interventions to "reverse" biomarker positivity. Clinicians express skepticism about diagnosing patients without comprehensive cognitive profiles, and media coverage has sharply criticized the profound pharmaceutical conflicts of interest among the workgroup authors.

## Caveats

- The guidelines explicitly recommend against diagnostic testing in cognitively unimpaired individuals outside of research, but early clinical adoption risks widespread misapplication and patient distress.
- These advanced biomarker testing frameworks are currently difficult to operationalize outside of major medical institutions or high-income countries.
- There remains a mismatch between this biological staging model, which includes asymptomatic individuals, and current FDA labels for anti-amyloid therapies that restrict use to symptomatic patients.
