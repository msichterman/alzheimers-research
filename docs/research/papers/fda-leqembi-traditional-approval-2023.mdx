## Takeaway

On July 6, 2023, the FDA granted traditional approval to Leqembi (lecanemab) for early Alzheimer's disease, making it the first anti-amyloid antibody to achieve full approval. The label includes a boxed warning for ARIA and recommends pre-treatment ApoE ε4 genetic testing.

## Key findings

- Phase 3 data showed a statistically significant reduction in clinical decline over 18 months on the CDR-SB compared to placebo (FDA press release).
- The drug carries a boxed warning for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), which can present as severe or fatal brain swelling and hemorrhage (FDA press release).
- ApoE ε4 homozygotes face a higher risk of symptomatic and severe ARIA, prompting a recommendation for genetic testing prior to initiation (FDA press release).

## Methods in brief

The approval relies on the Phase 3 CLARITY AD trial (Study 301), a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study spanning 18 months. The primary cohort included 1,795 patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild Alzheimer's disease dementia with confirmed amyloid pathology.

## Trial evidence

This regulatory action was driven by the Phase 3 [CLARITY AD](/trials/clarity-ad-nct03887455) trial (NCT03887455), which successfully confirmed the clinical benefit previously signaled in Phase 2. Note that while the FDA cites a cohort of 1,795, registry data indicates a final total enrollment of 1,906. Ongoing trials like [AHEAD 3-45](/trials/ahead-3-45) are currently evaluating the drug in earlier, preclinical stages.

## Public sentiment

Public and patient sentiment is highly mixed. While patient communities often view Leqembi with desperate hope, they report high distress over the grueling bi-weekly infusion schedule, the severe risks of ARIA, and the reality that decline continues despite treatment. Meanwhile, the biotech and investor communities remain skeptical of its commercial viability due to sluggish uptake, high costs, and widespread 'therapeutic nihilism' among prescribers.

## Caveats

- Efficacy and safety data is strictly limited to patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia; there is no evidence supporting initiation at earlier or later disease stages (FDA press release).
- Concurrent use of anticoagulant medications increases the risk of intracerebral hemorrhages (FDA press release).
