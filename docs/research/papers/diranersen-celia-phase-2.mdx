**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

Diranersen (BIIB080) is an investigational antisense therapy designed to halt tau production. While earlier Phase 1b testing successfully proved it reduces tau levels, Phase 2 (CELIA) clinical outcomes have reportedly been mixed, illustrating the difficulty of translating biomarker movement into cognitive benefit.

## Key findings

- Diranersen targets MAPT mRNA to prevent tau protein production ([NCT05399888](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05399888)).
- A preceding Phase 1b trial established safety and target engagement, reducing CSF total-tau by over 50% ([NCT03186989](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03186989)).
- The Phase 2 primary endpoint evaluates dose response on the CDR-SB over 76 weeks ([NCT05399888](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05399888)).

## Methods in brief

This Phase 2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 416 adults aged 50 to 80 with mild cognitive impairment or mild Alzheimer's dementia. Enrollment required confirmed amyloid pathology. Participants receive intrathecal injections of diranersen (low or high dose) or placebo every 12 or 24 weeks for 76 weeks, followed by a 96-week open-label extension. Registered as [NCT05399888](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05399888).

## Trial evidence

This document outlines the registry details for the pivotal [CELIA trial (NCT05399888)](/trials/diranersen-celia-nct05399888). It follows a successful Phase 1b trial ([NCT03186989](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03186989)) which demonstrated target engagement. The primary 76-week evaluation period for CELIA completed in March 2026. While official results are not yet posted to the registry, top-line clinical data recently circulated in investor and media reports following a July 2026 conference presentation.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is highly fragmented. Patients and caregivers maintain a quiet, cautious hope for tau-targeted therapies, recognizing the early stage of the research. In contrast, financial analysts and retail investors are heavily skeptical, focusing on mixed top-line clinical results and missed endpoints that triggered a stock slide despite optimistic corporate press releases.

## Caveats

- Enrollment strictly requires positive amyloid pathology, meaning these findings will not immediately generalize to primary tauopathies lacking amyloid.
- Current public discussions regarding clinical efficacy rely on press releases and conference coverage; full peer-reviewed data from the Phase 2 trial is pending.
