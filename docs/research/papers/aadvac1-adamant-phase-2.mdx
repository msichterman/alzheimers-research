## Takeaway

The active tau vaccine AADvac1 successfully induced high levels of IgG antibodies and proved safe over 24 months of treatment. However, it failed to significantly slow cognitive or functional decline in the broad mild Alzheimer's disease population. Subsequent development for this vaccine has shifted entirely to biomarker-stratified platform trials.

## Key findings

- AADvac1 was safe and well tolerated, with serious adverse events occurring in 17.1% of treated patients versus 24.1% on placebo (paper, Abstract).
- The vaccine induced high levels of IgG antibodies against tau (paper, Abstract).
- No significant cognitive benefit was observed in the overall cohort, with a Clinical Dementia Rating-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) adjusted mean difference of -0.360 (95% CI -1.306, 0.589) compared to placebo (paper, Abstract).
- No significant functional benefit was found, with a custom cognitive battery adjusted mean z-score difference of 0.0008 (95% CI -0.169, 0.172) (paper, Abstract).

## Methods in brief

This was a 24-month double-blind, parallel-arm, randomized Phase 2 multicenter placebo-controlled trial (EudraCT 2015-000630-30). It evaluated 196 patients with mild Alzheimer's disease dementia, randomized 3:2 to receive eleven 40 μg doses of AADvac1 (n=117) or placebo (n=79).

## Trial evidence

ADAMANT ([NCT02579252](/trials/aadvac1-adamant-nct02579252)) confirmed the safety and immunogenicity profile established in earlier Phase 1 trials (NCT01850238, NCT02031198). Following the failure to meet secondary clinical endpoints in this broad Phase 2 population, the clinical program pivoted. AADvac1 is now being evaluated in biomarker-stratified platform trials, including the Alzheimer's Tau Platform ([NCT07167966](/trials/atp-regimen-a-nct07167966)) and a PSP platform trial.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is mixed. Longevity and patient communities often express hope based on company messaging highlighting exploratory subgroup signals and proof-of-mechanism biomarker data. Conversely, the scientific and medical communities remain skeptical due to the trial missing its secondary cognitive endpoints and the sponsor's reliance on post hoc analyses to frame the vaccine as potentially efficacious.
