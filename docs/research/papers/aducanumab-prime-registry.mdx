**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2012](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01677572)
**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

The Phase 1b PRIME study evaluated the safety and amyloid-clearing ability of aducanumab in early Alzheimer's disease. The trial was terminated early in 2019—not for safety issues, but because the broader Phase 3 program (ENGAGE and EMERGE) hit a futility boundary, initiating a years-long controversy over the drug's efficacy.

## Key findings

- Enrolled 197 participants across multiple dosing arms to assess safety and amyloid reduction via PET imaging (registry).
- Terminated in July 2019 strictly due to external futility analyses of Phase 3 trials, not intrinsic safety signals (registry).

## Methods in brief

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multiple-dose Phase 1b trial (NCT01677572) enrolling 197 ambulatory adults (50-90 years) with prodromal or mild Alzheimer's disease and a positive amyloid PET scan. The core placebo-controlled period lasted 52 weeks, with a long-term extension originally planned up to week 518.

## Trial evidence

PRIME provided the foundational surrogate biomarker evidence (dose-dependent amyloid clearance) that ultimately supported aducanumab's controversial accelerated FDA approval in 2021. However, clinical efficacy remained deeply disputed due to contradictory results in the Phase 3 [ENGAGE](/trials/engage-nct02477800) and [EMERGE](/trials/emerge-nct02484547) trials. After failing to replicate convincing cognitive benefits and facing widespread market rejection, Biogen discontinued the entire Aduhelm program in January 2024.

## Public sentiment

Public and expert sentiment around aducanumab remains overwhelmingly negative, largely viewed as a cautionary tale of regulatory capture and premature commercialization. Initial patient hope quickly eroded due to high costs, severe ARIA side effects, unproven cognitive benefits, and Medicare coverage restrictions, culminating in widespread distrust following its 2024 market withdrawal.

## Caveats

- This document is a clinical registry record (NCT01677572) rather than a peer-reviewed publication, meaning outcome data and safety results are not detailed in the source text.
- The trial was designed primarily for safety and biomarker outcomes (PET imaging), so any potential clinical cognitive benefits observed during the study were exploratory.
