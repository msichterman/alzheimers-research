**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2019](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03887455)
**Authors**: Eisai Inc. and Biogen

## Takeaway

This document outlines the protocol for Clarity AD, the pivotal Phase 3 trial of lecanemab in early Alzheimer's disease. While the registry itself does not host the study's outcomes, the core 18-month trial ultimately met its primary endpoint—demonstrating a 27% slowing of clinical decline—and served as the basis for the drug's FDA approval.

## Key findings

- Evaluates lecanemab 10 mg/kg biweekly versus placebo over an 18-month core study period.
- Primary endpoint is the change from baseline in the Clinical Dementia Rating-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) at 18 months.
- The trial officially enrolled 1,906 participants with early AD and confirmed brain amyloid pathology.

## Methods in brief

Phase 3, randomized, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study. Enrolled 1,906 male and female participants aged 50-90 with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) or mild dementia due to Alzheimer's disease and confirmed amyloid pathology. The core study lasts 18 months, followed by an open-label extension phase lasting up to 66 months (NCT03887455).

## Trial evidence

This protocol describes [Clarity AD](/trials/lecanemab-clarity-ad), the Phase 3 confirmatory trial that demonstrated lecanemab's clinical efficacy, building on dose-finding data from the [Lecanemab Phase 2b](/research/papers/lecanemab-phase-2b) study. Lecanemab's efficacy in earlier disease stages is actively being evaluated in the [AHEAD 3-45](/trials/ahead-3-45) trial.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of lecanemab is starkly divided. While media outlets frequently frame the drug as a 'momentous breakthrough', patient and caregiver communities express significant concern over real-world risks like ARIA (brain swelling and bleeds), logistical infusion hurdles, and strict eligibility criteria that exclude many patients.

## Caveats

- This document is a trial registry protocol, not the peer-reviewed results publication; it outlines intended study parameters rather than actual clinical outcomes.
- The protocol strictly excludes individuals with more than 4 microhemorrhages, potentially limiting the generalizability of safety data to populations at higher baseline risk for ARIA.
- In certain countries, APOE4 homozygous carriers are excluded from the extension phase, which may obscure critical long-term safety data for the genetic subgroup most vulnerable to adverse events.
