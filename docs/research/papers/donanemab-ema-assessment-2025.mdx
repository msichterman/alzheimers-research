**Source**: [European Medicines Agency, 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu) — Procedure No. EMEA/H/C/006024/0000
**Authors**: Peter Mol et al. — Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP)

## Takeaway

Following an initial rejection in March 2025, the EMA approved donanemab by restricting its use to APOE4 heterozygotes and non-carriers. This subgroup analysis demonstrated a 19.6% to 28.5% slowing of clinical decline over 76 weeks, while halving the incidence of severe amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) compared to homozygotes. Treatment requires mandatory APOE4 testing and strict MRI monitoring.

## Key findings

- In APOE4 heterozygotes and non-carriers, donanemab slowed decline by 19.6% on the iADRS (2.65 points) and 28.5% on the CDR-SB (-0.69 points) versus placebo at 76 weeks using conservative missing-data imputation (Table 45).
- ARIA-E occurred in 15.7% of the restricted population versus 24.0% in the overall trial population, while ARIA-H occurred in 18.8% versus 31.4% (Table 28).
- A modified dose-titration regimen further reduced ARIA-E incidence to 14.7% in the restricted population and was adopted as an additional risk minimization measure (Table 59).
- Seven treatment-related deaths occurred across the clinical program, six of which were in APOE4 heterozygotes and one in a non-carrier, primarily due to ARIA or macrohemorrhages exacerbated by thrombolytics (Table 62).
- Treatment is limited to a maximum of 18 months, or until amyloid plaque clearance is confirmed via a validated method (Section 3.1.1).

## Methods in brief

This regulatory assessment evaluates data from the pivotal Phase 3 [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 (AACI)](/trials/trailblazer-alz-2-aaci) trial (n=1,736), the Phase 2 [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ (AACG)](/trials/trailblazer-alz-nct03367403) study, and the Phase 3b [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6 (AACQ)](/trials/trailblazer-alz-6-aacq) modified-titration study. Efficacy and safety conclusions rely on a post-hoc restricted subgroup of APOE4 heterozygotes and non-carriers (n=1,447 across datasets).

## Trial evidence

This document provides the definitive regulatory review of [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 (AACI)](/trials/trailblazer-alz-2-aaci), establishing its European approval basis. Notably, the EMA based its approval on a post-hoc genetic subgroup (APOE4 non-carriers and heterozygotes) and required conservative statistical imputation for safety-related dropouts, contrasting with the primary trial publications that stratified efficacy by tau levels.

## Public sentiment

Public response to the EMA's reversal is sharply divided. Investors view the approval as a significant commercial milestone, while the scientific community debates the clinical meaningfulness of the absolute point differences and the severe safety trade-offs. Patient communities express frustration over the strict APOE4 exclusions, which deny access to those at highest genetic risk.

## Caveats

- The approved efficacy estimates rely on post-hoc subgroup analyses (APOE4 heterozygotes and non-carriers) rather than the trial's prospectively defined tau-based populations.
- Absolute clinical benefits are modest (e.g., 0.69 points on the 18-point CDR-SB scale over 18 months), making individual patient benefit difficult to ascertain.
- Long-term outcomes after the mandatory 18-month treatment cessation remain uncertain, and accelerated brain volume loss observed in treated patients lacks a definitive mechanistic explanation.
- Fatalities from ARIA and macrohemorrhages still occurred in the restricted population, particularly when complicated by comorbid hypertension or concomitant thrombolytic therapy for ischemic stroke.

### Bias & spin flags

- Primary endpoint was changed from CDR-SB to the unvalidated iADRS scale, and sample size was increased, while the pivotal Phase 3 study was already ongoing.
- High dropout rate in the donanemab arm (29.3%) compared to placebo (20.1%), largely driven by ARIA adverse events.
- Efficacy in patients with high tau load was negligible, yet the final approved indication does not exclude this subgroup.
- Use of an 'enhanced titration' regimen as a post-hoc risk minimization measure was based on 52-week data from a separate study without corresponding 76-week efficacy data.

### Cross-examination

4 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Registry cross-check** — The document cites AACI-PC study efficacy outcomes specifically for the 'APOE4 non-carrier/heterozygote population', whereas the primary Phase 3 TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 registry results and JAMA publication report primary efficacy stratified by 'low/medium tau' and 'combined tau' populations; the APOE4-restricted analysis appears to be a post-hoc subgroup analysis prioritized by the EMA to establish a positive benefit-risk profile.
- **Registry cross-check** — The document claims the primary endpoint was changed from CDR-SB to iADRS while the pivotal Phase 3 study was already ongoing. However, ClinicalTrials.gov shows iADRS was the designated primary outcome measure upon the trial's initial public posting in June 2020, suggesting any protocol amendment changing the endpoint occurred before registry registration.
- **Misinformation watch** — Claiming donanemab reverses cognitive decline or restores lost memory.
- **Misinformation watch** — Assuming all Alzheimer's patients are eligible for the drug, ignoring the early-stage and APOE4 non-homozygote restrictions.
- **Misinformation watch** — Downplaying ARIA as a minor, asymptomatic side effect, despite reports of treatment-related fatalities.
