**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2018](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03444870)
**Authors**: Hoffmann-La Roche

## Takeaway

In the Phase 3 GRADUATE I trial, gantenerumab robustly cleared brain amyloid plaques and improved downstream biomarkers. However, it failed to demonstrate a statistically significant slowing of clinical decline on the primary endpoint (CDR-SB), leading to the termination of the gantenerumab development program.

## Key findings

- Gantenerumab did not significantly slow cognitive decline, showing a -0.31 difference on the CDR-SB at 116 weeks compared to placebo (p=0.0954) (Registry, Primary Outcome).
- The drug successfully engaged its target, reducing brain amyloid load by a difference of -66.44 centiloids versus placebo (p &lt; 0.0001) (Registry, Secondary Outcome).
- Treatment significantly improved downstream CSF biomarkers of neurodegeneration, including total tau, pTau-181, and neurogranin (p &lt; 0.001 for all) (Registry, Secondary Outcome).
- ARIA-E occurred more frequently in the treatment group (105 participants) compared to placebo (5 participants) (Registry, Secondary Outcome).
- Injection site reactions were more than twice as common in the treatment group (94 participants) than in the placebo group (43 participants) (Registry, Secondary Outcome).

## Methods in brief

Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolling 1,053 participants aged 50-90 with early (prodromal to mild) Alzheimer's disease and confirmed amyloid pathology. Participants received subcutaneous gantenerumab (titrated to 510 mg every two weeks) or placebo over a 116-week double-blind treatment period (NCT03444870).

## Trial evidence

GRADUATE I ([NCT03444870](/trials/gantenerumab-graduate-1-nct03444870)) read out negative simultaneously with its sister trial GRADUATE II. Together, they conclusively demonstrated that gantenerumab's amyloid clearance did not translate to clinical efficacy, confirming earlier futility signals seen in the SCarlet RoAD, Marguerite RoAD, and DIAN-TU-001 trials. Consequently, Roche terminated the entire gantenerumab clinical development program.

## Public sentiment

Public perception following the trial's termination is markedly negative. For patients and investors, it represented another significant setback in Alzheimer's drug development. Within the scientific community, the stark disconnect between gantenerumab's successful target engagement (amyloid clearance) and its lack of clinical benefit fueled intense debate and renewed skepticism regarding the amyloid cascade hypothesis.

## Caveats

- This page reflects raw registry data; the peer-reviewed analysis combining these results with the sister GRADUATE II trial provides the definitive evaluation of the program.
- The trial's China extension phase and long-term open-label extension were terminated early following the global futility results, leaving those endpoints incomplete.
