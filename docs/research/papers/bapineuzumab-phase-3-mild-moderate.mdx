**Source**: [The New England journal of medicine, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1304839) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24450891)
**Authors**: Salloway et al. — Janssen Alzheimer Immunotherapy and Pfizer

## Takeaway

Bapineuzumab did not slow cognitive or functional decline in patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. While the drug proved it could engage its biological targets—stabilizing brain amyloid and lowering CSF phospho-tau in APOE4 carriers—these changes failed to produce clinical benefits. The drug's narrow therapeutic window, driven by dose-dependent amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA-E), ultimately led to the termination of the highest dose and the eventual abandonment of the program.

## Key findings

- Bapineuzumab failed to improve primary cognitive (ADAS-cog11) or functional (DAD) scores at 78 weeks in either APOE4 carriers or noncarriers (paper, Abstract).
- The drug stabilized brain amyloid accumulation on PIB-PET and significantly reduced CSF phospho-tau in APOE4 carriers compared to placebo (paper, Key Biomarker Outcomes).
- ARIA-E occurred in 15.3% of bapineuzumab-treated carriers, reaching 27.3% in APOE4 homozygotes, compared to 0.2% on placebo (paper, Safety).
- Symptomatic ARIA-E rates forced the premature discontinuation of the highest evaluated dose (2.0 mg/kg) across the program (paper, Study Design and Treatment).

## Methods in brief

Two parallel double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials enrolled 2,452 patients aged 50 to 88 with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease (MMSE 16-26). One trial enrolled 1,121 APOE4 carriers; the other enrolled 1,331 noncarriers. Participants received intravenous bapineuzumab or placebo every 13 weeks for 78 weeks ([NCT00575055](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00575055), [NCT00574132](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00574132)).

## Trial evidence

These pivotal North American trials ([NCT00575055](/trials/bapineuzumab-nct00575055) and [NCT00574132](/trials/bapineuzumab-nct00574132)) conclusively demonstrated a lack of clinical efficacy for intravenous bapineuzumab. Consequently, sponsors terminated the entire clinical development program in August 2012, including parallel global trials ([NCT00676143](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00676143) and [NCT00667810](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00667810)).

## Public sentiment

Public perception is largely negative, heavily shaped by the severe financial losses investors suffered when these high-profile trials failed to meet primary endpoints. Within the scientific community, sentiment is more nuanced; researchers view the confirmed biomarker modifications as a critical biological proof-of-concept for the [amyloid hypothesis](/research/amyloid-hypothesis), arguing it proves the target was engaged but suggests interventions must happen earlier in the disease course.

## Caveats

- The premature discontinuation of the 2.0 mg/kg bapineuzumab dose severely limited the ability to evaluate whether higher-dose therapy could have driven clinical efficacy.
- Biomarker substudies for PIB-PET and CSF analysis relied on very small sample sizes, limiting the statistical power of the biological findings, particularly among noncarriers.

### Bias & spin flags

- More than a third (36.1%) of the noncarrier population tested negative for baseline amyloid pathology, casting serious doubt on the accuracy of clinical Alzheimer's diagnoses in this group and severely confounding the noncarrier efficacy data.
- The authors highlight prespecified sub-analyses using an alternative MMSE threshold (≥20) to find a marginal p&lt;0.05 difference in DAD scores for noncarriers, which risks presenting a spun interpretation of otherwise uniformly negative primary results.
