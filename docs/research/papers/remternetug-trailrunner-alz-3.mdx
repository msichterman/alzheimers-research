**Authors**: Eli Lilly and Company

## Takeaway

TRAILRUNNER-ALZ 3 is a large, multi-year Phase 3 trial testing whether subcutaneous remternetug can delay clinical progression in early Alzheimer's disease. With a primary completion timeline extending to 2030, it aims to establish long-term efficacy and safety for this next-generation administration route.

## Key findings

- The trial targets individuals aged 55-80 with biomarker-confirmed amyloid pathology but minimal to no current cognitive impairment (source: Eligibility).
- The primary endpoint evaluates the time to clinically meaningful progression on the Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) scale over up to 255 weeks (source: Primary outcome measures).
- Participants are randomized to receive either subcutaneous remternetug or a placebo (source: Arms and Interventions).

## Methods in brief

This is a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolling an estimated 1,400 adults aged 55 to 80. Participants must have P-tau levels consistent with amyloid pathology but minimal cognitive and functional impairment. The study spans up to 255 weeks to evaluate time to clinical progression.

## Trial evidence

This trial evaluates the long-term clinical efficacy of remternetug, following the earlier [TRAILRUNNER-ALZ 1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05463731) trial, which focused on amyloid clearance and self-administration feasibility. It serves as a pivotal study to confirm whether subcutaneous delivery provides sustained disease modification and delays cognitive decline.

## Public sentiment

Public sentiment is highly optimistic, driven by patient and caregiver hopes that a subcutaneous injection will eliminate the burden of IV infusions and enable simple at-home treatment. Retail investors also view this delivery method as a competitive advantage for Eli Lilly. However, mainstream biotech media remains cautious, pointing to high ARIA (brain swelling) rates in earlier anti-amyloid data that complicate the vision of unmonitored at-home use.

## Caveats

- This document outlines study design and protocols for a trial that recently began; it does not contain clinical results.
- Despite public hopes for near-term availability, this specific Phase 3 trial is not scheduled to complete until October 2030.
- Enthusiasm for casual at-home administration may outpace reality if the drug requires rigorous MRI monitoring for ARIA, similar to other anti-amyloid therapies.
