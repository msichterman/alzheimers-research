**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2025](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07170150)
**Authors**: Hoffmann-La Roche

## Takeaway

Roche is advancing its Brainshuttle anti-amyloid antibody, trontinemab, into a massive Phase 3 program. The TRONTIER 2 trial will evaluate cognitive outcomes over 72 weeks, seeking to prove that enhanced blood-brain barrier penetration translates to superior clinical efficacy.

## Key findings

- The primary endpoint is change in the Clinical Dementia Rating-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) at Week 72 (registry record).
- The target population requires confirmed amyloid pathology and a screening MMSE score of 22 or higher (registry record).

## Methods in brief

Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial. Enrolling approximately 800 adults aged 50 to 90 with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease. Treatment duration is 72 weeks.

## Trial evidence

[TRONTIER 2](/trials/trontinemab-nct07170150) is part of a broad late-stage clinical program for trontinemab. The rapid progression to Phase 3 prior to final Phase 1/2 peer-reviewed publication suggests robust interim biomarker data from the ongoing [Brainshuttle AD](/trials/trontinemab-nct04639050) trial.

## Public sentiment

Public and investor sentiment is highly optimistic, driven by early Phase 1b/2a biomarker data showing rapid amyloid clearance. However, enthusiasm sometimes outpaces evidence, with some retail investors prematurely assuming these biomarker improvements guarantee superior cognitive slowing before Phase 3 trials read out.

## Caveats

- This document is a prospective registry protocol, not a peer-reviewed report of clinical results.
- The relationship between rapid amyloid clearance via Brainshuttle delivery and actual cognitive benefit remains unproven until Phase 3 completion.
