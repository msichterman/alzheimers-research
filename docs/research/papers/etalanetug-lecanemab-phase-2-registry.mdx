**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2024](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06602258)
**Authors**: Eisai Inc.

## Takeaway

This ongoing Phase 2 trial is evaluating the safety and biomarker efficacy of combining the experimental anti-tau antibody etalanetug (E2814) with the approved anti-amyloid lecanemab. As a registry record, no clinical results are available yet, but it marks an important step toward multi-target combination therapies.

## Key findings

- No clinical findings are available; the trial is currently active but not recruiting, with primary completion expected in December 2026.

## Methods in brief

Phase 2, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-finding trial. Enrolled 105 participants aged 50-80 with early Alzheimer's disease (MCI or mild dementia). Evaluates four doses of intravenous etalanetug versus placebo, all administered concurrently with weekly subcutaneous lecanemab, over up to 27 months.

## Trial evidence

Etalanetug (E2814) has demonstrated target engagement and adequate safety in Phase 1 trials ([NCT04971733](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04971733), [NCT04231513](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04231513)). This [Phase 2 trial](/trials/etalanetug-nct06602258) is the first to test its efficacy in sporadic early AD alongside lecanemab, paralleling the ongoing DIAN-TU Tau platform trial in dominantly inherited AD.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is highly segmented. Scientists and retail investors show enthusiasm driven by an FDA Fast Track designation and early biomarker reductions in dominantly inherited AD. Meanwhile, patient and caregiver communities remain largely unaware of etalanetug, focusing instead on already-approved monotherapies.

## Caveats

- This is a registry record, not a completed study, meaning efficacy and safety for this combination in sporadic Alzheimer's remain completely unproven.
- Current investor and scientific enthusiasm relies heavily on extrapolating early, small-cohort biomarker data from dominantly inherited Alzheimer's to the broader sporadic disease population.
