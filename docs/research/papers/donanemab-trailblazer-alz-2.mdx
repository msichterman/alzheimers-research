**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2020](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04437511) · [JAMA, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.13239)
**Authors**: Eli Lilly and Company (Sponsor)

## Takeaway

In a pivotal Phase 3 trial, donanemab significantly slowed clinical decline and robustly cleared brain amyloid plaques over 76 weeks in patients with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease. This biomarker and clinical efficacy supported its 2024 FDA approval, though the drug is accompanied by a substantial risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA).

## Key findings

- Donanemab slowed clinical decline compared to placebo on the iADRS score (difference of +2.92 points, p&lt;0.001) in the overall population (ClinicalTrials.gov).
- Treatment profoundly reduced brain amyloid plaque burden by an average of 86.37 Centiloids compared to placebo (ClinicalTrials.gov).
- ARIA-E (edema) occurred in 23.4% (200/853) of donanemab participants versus 1.9% (17/874) on placebo, and ARIA-H (microhemorrhages) occurred in 19.5% vs. 7.4% (ClinicalTrials.gov).
- Infusion-related reactions were noticeably higher in the donanemab arm at 8.6% (73/853) compared to 0.5% (4/874) for placebo (ClinicalTrials.gov).

## Methods in brief

TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 was a Phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial enrolling 1,736 participants aged 60 to 85. Eligible patients had early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (prodromal to mild dementia) with positive amyloid and tau PET scans. Participants received intravenous donanemab or placebo every four weeks for up to 76 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This study provides the pivotal evidence for donanemab, corroborating earlier Phase 2 findings and establishing robust proof of clinical and biomarker efficacy. The statistically significant slowing of decline directly led to the FDA approval of Kisunla (donanemab-azbt) in July 2024. The safety profile is consistent with the established class-effect risks of anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of donanemab is heavily polarized. Retail investors and mainstream headlines often frame the drug enthusiastically as a major therapeutic breakthrough, while patients and caregivers balance a desperate hope for delayed disease progression against significant anxiety regarding ARIA and logistical burdens (like MRI monitoring). Scientists and medical professionals maintain a more measured stance, weighing the impressive plaque clearance against the modest clinical benefit and serious safety risks.

## Caveats

- The clinical meaningfulness of a 2.92-point change on the 144-point iADRS scale over 18 months remains heavily debated among clinicians.
- The registry extract lacks demographic data, making it difficult to assess how well the findings generalize across diverse populations.
- While deaths directly attributed to the drug are not fully detailed in the registry top-line, the higher overall rate of ARIA practically necessitates strict, burdensome monitoring protocols in real-world clinical use.
