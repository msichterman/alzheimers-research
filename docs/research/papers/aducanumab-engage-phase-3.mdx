**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

The ENGAGE trial demonstrated that aducanumab failed to slow cognitive or functional decline in early Alzheimer's disease. Terminated early for futility alongside its sister trial EMERGE, the study revealed a high incidence of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) without accompanying clinical benefit.

## Key findings

- No significant difference from placebo on the primary endpoint (CDR-SB) at week 78 (difference 0.110, p=0.2250) ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)).
- No significant benefit on secondary global cognitive and functional measures, including the MMSE, ADAS-Cog 13, and ADCS-ADL-MCI ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)).
- Brain swelling (ARIA-E) occurred in 35% of high-dose participants compared to just 3% of those on placebo ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)).
- Microhemorrhages (ARIA-H) affected 18.3% of the high-dose group versus 6.3% of the placebo group ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)).

## Methods in brief

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial enrolling 1,653 adults (ages 50-85) with early Alzheimer's disease and positive amyloid PET scans. Participants received monthly intravenous infusions of low-dose aducanumab, high-dose aducanumab, or placebo for up to 78 weeks ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)).

## Trial evidence

ENGAGE ([NCT02477800](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)) and its sister trial EMERGE ([NCT02484547](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02484547)) were halted simultaneously for futility. However, Biogen subsequently conducted a highly controversial post-hoc analysis of EMERGE to claim efficacy in a high-dose subset. This led to an unprecedented FDA accelerated approval that circumvented the nearly unanimous rejection by FDA advisory committees. The drug was ultimately discontinued following EMA rejection, severe safety risks, and poor commercial reception.

## Public sentiment

Public and professional perception of aducanumab is highly polarized and predominantly skeptical. While patient communities expressed a desperate hope for treatments, they remained wary of ARIA risks and insurance barriers. The scientific and regulatory communities broadly condemned the FDA's accelerated approval as a regulatory failure driven by statistical manipulation, prioritizing biomarker changes over the definitive lack of clinical benefit observed in ENGAGE.

## Caveats

- The trial was terminated prematurely, truncating the planned 78-week dataset and forcing reliance on an interim futility analysis.
- As a raw registry export, the data lacks the nuanced statistical context, methodology, and subgroup analyses that typically accompany peer-reviewed publications.
