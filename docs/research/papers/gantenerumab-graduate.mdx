**Source**: [The New England Journal of Medicine, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37966285)
**Authors**: R.J. Bateman et al. — F. Hoffmann-La Roche

## Takeaway

Gantenerumab failed to significantly slow cognitive decline in early Alzheimer's disease over 116 weeks. While the drug reduced brain amyloid and shifted some fluid biomarkers toward normal, the magnitude of plaque clearance was surprisingly weak, with roughly a quarter of patients achieving amyloid negativity ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430)).

## Key findings

- Did not significantly slow decline on the CDR-SB compared to placebo in either GRADUATE I (difference −0.31, P=0.10) or GRADUATE II (difference −0.19, P=0.30) ([paper, Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430)).
- Only 28.0% (GRADUATE I) and 26.8% (GRADUATE II) of treated participants reached amyloid-negative status by week 116 ([paper, Results](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430)).
- ARIA-E occurred in 24.9% of treated participants versus 2.7% on placebo, with 5.0% experiencing symptomatic ARIA-E ([paper, Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430)).
- Treated patients showed improvements in downstream fluid biomarkers, including lower CSF p-tau181 and higher Aβ42 ([paper, Results](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2304430)).

## Methods in brief

Two Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. Enrolled 1,965 participants aged 50 to 90 with early Alzheimer's disease and evidence of amyloid plaques. Evaluated subcutaneous gantenerumab versus placebo over 116 weeks ([NCT03444870](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03444870), [NCT03443973](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03443973)).

## Trial evidence

These trials mark the definitive failure of the gantenerumab clinical program. Following negative readouts in [GRADUATE I](/trials/gantenerumab-graduate-1-nct03444870) and [GRADUATE II](/trials/gantenerumab-graduate-2-nct03443973), Hoffmann-La Roche formally terminated the studies. Earlier trials (SCarlet RoAD and Marguerite RoAD) had similarly failed to show clinical benefit, and the gantenerumab arm of the DIAN-TU prevention trial was subsequently discontinued.

## Public sentiment

Public and market sentiment is highly skeptical. Retail investors view the failure as a major setback for Roche, while patient communities express frustration that clearing amyloid did not translate to memory improvements, fueling distrust in the [amyloid hypothesis](/journey/amyloid-hypothesis-consumer) and heightening fears around ARIA risks. Scientific focus has largely shifted toward trontinemab, a next-generation "brain-shuttle" drug designed to solve gantenerumab's delivery limitations.

## Caveats

- The lack of racial diversity in the US trial cohort limits the generalizability of the findings to broader populations.
- A novel dose-escalation scheme was used to manage ARIA-E, making direct comparisons with earlier gantenerumab studies difficult.
- Because amyloid removal was much lower than predicted, the trials could not definitively test whether profound plaque clearance yields clinical benefit.
