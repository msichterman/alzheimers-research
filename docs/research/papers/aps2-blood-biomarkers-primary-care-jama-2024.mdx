**Source**: [JAMA, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.13855) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39068545)
**Authors**: Palmqvist et al. — Skane University Hospital

## Takeaway

The APS2 blood test (combining p-tau217 and Aβ42/40 ratios) accurately detects Alzheimer's disease pathology in approximately 90% of patients with cognitive symptoms. It significantly outperforms standard diagnostic evaluations by both primary care physicians and dementia specialists, highlighting its potential to dramatically improve and streamline Alzheimer's diagnosis in routine practice.

## Key findings

- The APS2 blood test demonstrated 88% to 92% diagnostic accuracy for Alzheimer's pathology across primary and secondary care cohorts (JAMA abstract).
- Standard clinical evaluation yielded a diagnostic accuracy of only 61% for primary care physicians and 73% for dementia specialists, compared to 91% using the APS2 test (JAMA abstract).
- The percentage of p-tau217 alone achieved a diagnostic accuracy of 90%, showing no statistical difference from the combined APS2 score (JAMA abstract).

## Methods in brief

A prospective cohort study of 1,213 patients (mean age 74.2 years, 48% women) undergoing clinical evaluation for cognitive symptoms in primary and secondary care settings in Sweden. The study ran from February 2020 to January 2024, evaluating predefined cutoff values for the APS2 blood test.

## Trial evidence

This paper reports findings from the Swedish BioFINDER primary care ([NCT06120361](/trials/biofinder-primary-care-nct06120361)) and secondary care ([NCT03174938](/trials/biofinder-2-nct03174938)) cohorts. The prospective evidence strongly supports the clinical utility of the APS2 biomarker (commercially available as PrecivityAD2) for detecting Alzheimer's pathology. The test is increasingly being integrated into both therapeutic endpoint strategies and dedicated diagnostic workflow trials.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is overwhelmingly hopeful, heavily influenced by media headlines emphasizing the test's high accuracy over human doctors ([HealthDay News](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/neurology/blood-test-91-accurate-at-predicting-alzheimers-outperforming-doctors)). However, patient communities and the broader public frequently misinterpret the test as a standalone "lie-detector" for clinical dementia or an asymptomatic screening tool, rather than a measure of pathology that must be contextualized with observed cognitive symptoms.

## Caveats

- The blood test detects the presence of underlying Alzheimer's pathology (amyloid and tau), which does not automatically equate to a clinical diagnosis of dementia without corresponding cognitive symptoms.
- The available text is limited to the abstract, obscuring detailed methodological limitations, specific demographic diversity of the Swedish cohorts, and full financial disclosures of the authors.
