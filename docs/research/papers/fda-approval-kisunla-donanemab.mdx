## Takeaway

The FDA approved donanemab (Kisunla) but requires extensive postmarketing surveillance due to severe risks of ARIA and intracerebral hemorrhage. Eli Lilly must establish a large observational registry, a retrospective cohort study, a trial for maintenance dosing, and an FDA-cleared in vitro diagnostic for ApoE ε4 detection.

## Key findings

- FDA mandates a prospective registry to evaluate real-world rates of ARIA and intracerebral hemorrhage (>1 cm), particularly targeting high-risk groups like ApoE ε4 homozygotes (Letter, p. 5).
- The sponsor must validate administrative claim codes and conduct a retrospective cohort study to assess clinical safety outcomes using real-world data (Letter, p. 6).
- The FDA requires a randomized, double-blind controlled trial to evaluate maintenance dosing of donanemab versus placebo for at least two years after initial amyloid plaque clearance (Letter, p. 8).
- An FDA-cleared in vitro diagnostic (IVD) device must be validated by July 2026 to accurately detect ApoE ε4 alleles, helping to identify patients at elevated risk for ARIA (Letter, p. 8).

## Methods in brief

This is a regulatory approval letter (BLA 761248) issued July 2, 2024, outlining postmarketing requirements (PMRs) and postmarketing commitments (PMCs) under section 505(o) of the FDCA for adult Alzheimer's patients.

## Trial evidence

The approval rests on efficacy and safety data from the pivotal TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 Phase 3 trial ([NCT04437511](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04437511)). To satisfy the new postmarketing surveillance mandates, a prospective observational registry is currently recruiting up to 6,250 participants ([NCT06566170](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06566170)). The mandated maintenance dosing trial (PMC 4605-3) and retrospective claims study (PMR 4605-2) do not yet have distinct active ClinicalTrials.gov registrations, though the maintenance trial may incorporate crossover participants from the ongoing [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3](/trials/trailblazer-alz-3) prevention trial.

## Public sentiment

Public sentiment toward Kisunla is deeply mixed. Patient and caregiver communities weigh the drug's modest clinical benefit against severe side effects like ARIA and allergic reactions. The medical community emphasizes a narrow risk-benefit profile, while retail investors largely ignore the Alzheimer's portfolio in favor of the sponsor's GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. Expectations among some patients are occasionally inflated by misinformation suggesting the drug can halt the disease for a decade, contrasting sharply with the roughly 4-month delay in progression observed in trials.

## Caveats

- This document outlines regulatory mandates and safety commitments; it does not present primary clinical trial data on efficacy or adverse event frequencies.
- The specific designs and endpoints of the mandated retrospective cohort study and maintenance dosing trial remain subject to FDA review and have not yet been published.
