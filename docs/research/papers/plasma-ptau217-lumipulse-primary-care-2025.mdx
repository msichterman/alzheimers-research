**Authors**: Sebastian Palmqvist et al. — Skåne University Hospital / Lund University

## Takeaway

The fully automated Lumipulse plasma p-tau217 test accurately detects Alzheimer's pathology in secondary (89-91%) and primary (85%) care settings. However, lower accuracy in patients over 80 necessitates a two-cutoff approach, leaving up to 17% of patients in an intermediate zone requiring confirmatory testing.

## Key findings

- Demonstrated single-cutoff accuracies of 89–91% in secondary care and 85% in primary care ([paper, Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03622-w)).
- Diagnostic accuracy significantly declined in older patients, dropping to 83% for those aged 80 and above, while remaining unaffected by chronic kidney disease or cognitive stage ([paper, Results](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03622-w)).
- Implementing a two-cutoff approach improved overall accuracy to 92–94% but classified 12–17% of participants into an intermediate zone requiring further testing ([paper, Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03622-w)).
- Using the p-tau217:Aβ42 ratio did not improve overall accuracy but reduced the number of intermediate results to 7–10% ([paper, Results](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03622-w)).

## Methods in brief

Retrospective cross-sectional analysis of 1,767 participants with cognitive symptoms (subjective cognitive decline, mild cognitive impairment, or dementia) across four secondary care cohorts and one primary care cohort. The study pooled data collected between April 2017 and November 2023, drawing from observational registries including [BioFINDER-Memory Clinic](/trials/validate-biofinder-memory-clinic-nct06122415) and [BioFINDER-Primary Care](/trials/biofinder-primary-care-nct06120361).

## Trial evidence

This paper reports interim cross-sectional diagnostic accuracy from two ongoing prospective observational cohorts: [BioFINDER-Memory Clinic](/trials/validate-biofinder-memory-clinic-nct06122415) (NCT06122415) and [BioFINDER-Primary Care](/trials/biofinder-primary-care-nct06120361) (NCT06120361). Both studies are actively recruiting to evaluate the real-world utility of blood-based biomarkers. As noted by the analyst, this represents an interim evaluation of enrolled participants rather than final longitudinal trial results.

## Public sentiment

Mainstream media coverage is highly enthusiastic, framing the test as a revolutionary and accessible tool for democratizing Alzheimer's diagnosis in primary care. However, the medical community and science-literate public express more cautious optimism, noting that the required two-cutoff system and intermediate results mean the test cannot entirely replace PET scans or lumbar punctures, particularly for patients over 80.

## Caveats

- The study population was exclusively European, requiring further validation of the cutoffs in diverse global cohorts.
- The automated Lumipulse assay performed slightly worse than mass spectrometry-based methods in primary care and older populations.

5 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Registry cross-check** — The document lists NCT06122415 and NCT06120361 under 'registration' which could imply these are completed studies, but the registry records for both BioFINDER cohorts list them as currently 'Recruiting' with expected completions in 2026 and 2028; the published paper represents an interim cross-sectional readout of participants enrolled to date rather than final longitudinal trial results.
