**Source**: [Nature Genetics, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01024-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35379992)
**Authors**: Bellenguez et al. — European Alzheimer & Dementia Biobank (EADB)

## Takeaway

This two-stage GWAS meta-analysis of over 780,000 individuals identified 75 risk loci for Alzheimer's disease, including 42 novel regions. The genetic data strongly implicates microglia, the TNF-alpha pathway, and the LUBAC complex in disease pathogenesis. A polygenic risk score based on these findings effectively predicts progression from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's disease independently of APOE status.

## Key findings

- 75 independent risk loci were associated with AD, 42 of which were newly discovered ([paper, Table 1/2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01024-z)).
- Single-cell enrichment analysis demonstrated a highly significant association between AD risk and microglial gene expression (P = 1.7 × 10^-8).
- Gene prioritization highlighted the TNF-alpha signaling pathway and the linear ubiquitin chain assembly complex (LUBAC) via genes like SHARPIN, RBCK1, and OTULIN.
- A novel genetic risk score (GRS) increased AD risk by 1.6- to 1.9-fold from the lowest to highest decile, predicting disease progression independently of age and APOE status.

## Methods in brief

Two-stage Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) meta-analysis evaluating 111,326 clinically diagnosed and 'proxy' AD cases against 677,663 controls of European ancestry. The study utilized a longitudinal follow-up of up to 10 years for prospective cohort analysis to validate the genetic risk score.

## Trial evidence

While this genetic study strongly links microglia and the TNF-alpha pathway to Alzheimer's risk, clinical trials directly targeting these mechanisms have yet to succeed. The TREM2 agonizing antibody AL002 recently failed the Phase 2 [INVOKE-2](/trials/al002-invoke-2) trial, terminating its open-label extension. Similarly, trials evaluating TNF-alpha inhibitors like etanercept and pegipanermin ([XPro1595-AD-02](/trials/pegipanermin-xpro1595-ad-02)) have either failed to show cognitive benefit or faced development hurdles, suggesting these genetic risk pathways may be difficult to modulate therapeutically once disease is established.

## Public sentiment

Scientific communities and media outlets responded enthusiastically to the scale of the findings and the predictive power of the new genetic risk score. However, patient communities exhibit anxiety around genetic determinism, with some mistakenly interpreting consumer DNA data mapped to these 75 loci as a definitive Alzheimer's diagnosis rather than a probabilistic risk factor.

## Caveats

- The study cohorts included 'proxy-ADD' cases defined merely by family history questionnaires, introducing phenotypic heterogeneity compared to clinical or pathological diagnoses.
- The genetic risk score was constructed and validated predominantly in European-ancestry populations, severely limiting its predictive accuracy for other ancestries.
