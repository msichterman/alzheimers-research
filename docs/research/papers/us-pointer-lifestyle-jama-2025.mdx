**Authors**: Baker et al.

## Takeaway

The US-POINTER trial found that a structured multidomain lifestyle intervention (including the MIND diet and regular exercise) yielded a statistically significant cognitive benefit over a self-guided version. However, the absolute difference was extremely small (0.029 SD/year), and cognitive scores inexplicably increased from baseline in both groups. This strongly suggests the cognitive tests were measuring practice effects rather than genuine neurobiological enhancement.

## Key findings

- Mean global cognitive scores increased over the 2-year study in both the structured (+0.243 SD/year) and self-guided (+0.213 SD/year) groups (paper, Results).
- The structured intervention showed a statistically significant but clinically negligible greater improvement of 0.029 SD/year compared to the self-guided approach (paper, Results).
- The observed benefit was consistent regardless of APOE ε4 carrier status but appeared slightly larger for those starting with lower baseline cognition (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

A single-blind, multicenter randomized clinical trial enrolling 2,111 older adults (ages 60-79) at risk of cognitive decline due to sedentary lifestyles and suboptimal diets. Participants were randomly assigned to either a structured (n=1056) or self-guided (n=1055) 2-year lifestyle intervention (paper, Design). Registered as [NCT03688126](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03688126).

## Trial evidence

US-POINTER ([NCT03688126](/trials/us-pointer-nct03688126)) represents a major effort to validate the effects of diet, exercise, and cognitive challenges on dementia risk. However, the results are heavily confounded by the lack of a true no-intervention control arm and pervasive practice effects, making it difficult to demonstrate genuine disease modification. These findings follow similar trials like FINGER (which showed modest protection) and MAPT (which showed no significant benefit).

## Public sentiment

Public perception often overestimates the ability of diet and exercise to halt or reverse Alzheimer's disease, fueled by overly optimistic media narratives. Patient and caregiver communities remain highly invested in lifestyle modifications as empowering tools, but scientific circles emphasize the clinical insignificance of the trial's marginal cognitive difference and point to practice effects as the likely cause of the score improvements.

## Caveats

- Because cognitive test scores improved over two years in both study arms, participants likely just got better at taking the tests over time (practice effects).
- The 0.029 standard deviation annual difference between the two groups is mathematically significant but practically invisible in a real-world clinical setting.
- The trial currently lacks functional outcomes or biomarker data to prove the intervention altered underlying disease pathology.
