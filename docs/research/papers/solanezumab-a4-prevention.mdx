## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: not_met.** The primary end point — change in PACC score over 240 weeks in cognitively unimpaired, amyloid-positive participants — showed no significant between-group difference (difference, −0.30; 95% CI, −0.82 to 0.22; P = 0.26), with numerically greater decline in the solanezumab group.
> which indicated no significant between-group difference and a numerically greater decline in the solanezumab group
**Multiplicity handling:** Prespecified graphical testing scheme for the primary and secondary end points, with change in the PACC score as the primary end point followed by the secondary end points; because the primary end point failed, no claims of significance were allowed for subsequent end points. Imaging end points were reported with multiplicity-unadjusted confidence intervals only.

### Risk of bias

- **attrition / missing primary outcome data** — Roughly 30% of participants in each group were missing the final-visit PACC observation, the primary end-point measure; the sample-size calculation had assumed 30% attrition and the model used all available observations without imputation.
- **sponsor involvement in design and analysis** — The sponsor (Eli Lilly) funded the trial, supplied drug and placebo, held the IND, contributed three author-employees to the trial design, and ran parallel analyses; the document states the academic team coordinated the trial and that authors were unrestricted in data access and publication.
- **mid-trial protocol change (dose increase and duration extension)** — The dose was quadrupled and the double-blind phase extended after roughly 970 participants had already been enrolled, so exposure differed across the cohort; the authors list this among the trial's limitations.
- **analysis-plan amendment** — The primary analysis method was changed from mixed-model repeated measures to a constrained longitudinal spline analysis using days since baseline; the document states this was done before unblinding.
- **selection / representativeness of the enrolled population** — Self-referred volunteers recruited via advertising and registries, with Black and Hispanic or Latino participants underrepresented relative to the general population, limiting generalizability.
- **external disruption (Covid-19)** — Pandemic social-distancing measures interrupted dosing and testing and pushed many visits past their target dates, forcing the change in the time variable used for analysis.
- **internal reporting consistency** — The Results section reports 33.4% of participants across both groups with progression of the global CDR score at 240 weeks, while the Discussion reports progression to more than 0 in 36% of participants.

### Limitations stated by the document

- Participants were not representative of older persons at risk for cognitive decline, and there were few Black participants.
- Our trial was complicated by a midtrial dose increase.
- The trial had widespread disruption of activities at the sites due to the Covid-19 pandemic.
- For the amyloid PET results: statistical inferences from these results have limitations, and only multiplicity-unadjusted 95% confidence intervals are provided for imaging end-point analyses, which cannot be used for statistical inferences.
- For the post hoc baseline-amyloid analysis: there was no prespecified plan for adjustment of the widths of confidence intervals for multiple comparisons, and no conclusions can be drawn from this analysis.
- No formal statistical conclusions can be drawn from the analyses of baseline amyloid level and its association with decline and progression.

### Where authoritative sources disagree

*Stored as structured evidence with each position attributed to its source, not resolved into a single verdict.*

- **Does slowing amyloid accumulation, without reducing fibrillar amyloid below baseline, slow clinical progression in cognitively unimpaired amyloid-positive people?** — It does not; the A4 results are consistent with trials showing that only substantial reduction of fibrillar amyloid toward normal levels is associated with slowed progression. *(the authors of this trial report (A4 investigators))*
- **Does solanezumab produce the cognitive benefit suggested by the meta-analysis of the EXPEDITION trials in mild Alzheimer's dementia?** — This trial found numerical worsening on the primary and secondary end points with solanezumab, which the authors call unexpected given the EXPEDITION meta-analysis, and they conjecture the fourfold dose increase may have severely depleted monomeric Aβ and yielded cognitive worsening. *(the authors of this trial report (A4 investigators), contrasting with the EXPEDITION meta-analysis)*
- **Does the accumulation of brain amyloid in a cognitively unimpaired population mark early-stage Alzheimer's disease?** — Yes — observed cognitive and clinical decline plus tau accumulation on flortaucipir PET are consistent with elevated amyloid in an unimpaired population being a marker for early-stage Alzheimer's disease. *(the authors of this trial report (A4 investigators))*
