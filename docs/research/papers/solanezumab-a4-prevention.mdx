**Authors**: Reisa A. Sperling et al. — A4 Study Team

## Takeaway

Solanezumab, an antibody targeting monomeric amyloid, failed to slow cognitive decline in older adults with preclinical Alzheimer's disease over 4.5 years. The drug slightly slowed new amyloid accumulation but could not clear existing plaques, confirming that targeting amyloid monomers does not provide clinical benefit.

## Key findings

- Solanezumab did not slow cognitive decline; the treatment group actually showed a numerically greater decline than placebo (PACC score change −1.43 vs. −1.13, P=0.26) ([paper, RESULTS](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2305032)).
- Brain amyloid levels continued to increase in both groups, though slightly less with solanezumab (+11.6 centiloids) than placebo (+19.3 centiloids).
- ARIA with edema (ARIA-E) occurred in less than 1% of participants in both groups, distinguishing it from plaque-clearing antibodies.

## Methods in brief

Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (A4). Enrolled 1,169 cognitively unimpaired adults (ages 65 to 85) with elevated brain amyloid on PET scans. Participants received intravenous solanezumab or placebo every 4 weeks for 240 weeks. Registered as [NCT02008357](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02008357).

## Trial evidence

The A4 trial ([NCT02008357](/trials/solanezumab-a4-nct02008357)) represents the definitive clinical failure of solanezumab. It follows a string of negative results across the disease continuum, including mild-to-moderate AD (EXPEDITION 1 and 2), mild AD (EXPEDITION 3), and dominantly inherited AD (DIAN-TU). Together, these findings led to the complete discontinuation of solanezumab for Alzheimer's disease.

## Public sentiment

Public and market sentiment regarding solanezumab is quiet, as the drug's failure was widely expected following previous trial misses. Within the scientific community, the A4 study is viewed constructively as definitive proof that targeting monomeric amyloid is an ineffective mechanism, thereby validating the field's pivot toward plaque-clearing therapies.

## Caveats

- The trial was severely disrupted midway through by both a drastic protocol change (quadrupling the drug dose) and the COVID-19 pandemic, which forced researchers to change how time and visits were analyzed in their statistical models.
- The participant population lacked racial and ethnic diversity and was highly educated, which limits how reliably these findings generalize to the broader aging population.
