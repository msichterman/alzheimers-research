## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: not_met.** The primary endpoint, change from baseline to Week 96 in CDR-SB, was not met: no tilavonemab dose differed significantly from placebo (300 mg −0.07, 95% CI −0.83 to 0.69; 1000 mg −0.06, 95% CI −0.81 to 0.68; 2000 mg 0.16, 95% CI −0.60 to 0.93; all P ≥ 0.05), and CDR-SB worsened in every group.
> Tilavonemab was generally well tolerated but did not demonstrate efficacy in treating patients with early Alzheimer’s disease. Further investigations of tilavonemab in early Alzheimer’s disease are not warranted.
**Multiplicity handling:** Bonferroni method to adjust multiplicity, with one-sided testing at the 2.5% significance level, in the powering and testing framework for the primary CDR-SB endpoint; the exploratory hippocampal volume comparisons are explicitly described as being without multiplicity adjustment, and the age subgroup analysis was adjusted for multiple comparisons across the three dose groups

### Risk of bias

- **sponsor analysis** — The sponsor funded the trial and participated in design, research, collection, analysis and interpretation of the data, and most authors are AbbVie employees who may hold AbbVie stock.
- **multiplicity / selective inference** — The only positive imaging findings (hippocampal volume at Week 28 and Week 44) are nominal, at isolated time points, and explicitly without multiplicity adjustment.
- **interim analyses / data monitoring committee access to efficacy data** — Three interim efficacy analyses were performed and the data monitoring committee reviewed unblinded efficacy data during the trial.
- **attrition / missing data** — Analysis of the primary endpoint at Week 96 rested on 85, 91 and 81 patients in the three tilavonemab groups out of 108, 116 and 113 randomized, with missing data handled by multiple imputation applied separately within treatment groups.
- **external validity / generalizability** — The trial population was overwhelmingly White and well educated, which the document itself flags as a limitation.
- **incomplete biomarker ascertainment** — Tau PET was added after the study commenced and only a subset of patients contributed scans, limiting the assessment of the drug's effect on tau accumulation.

### Limitations stated by the document

- The natural history of the disease in the study population: patients clinically diagnosed with early Alzheimer's disease may already be too advanced pathologically to benefit from an anti-tau antibody
- Tau imaging was not available when the trial began, so the effect of tilavonemab on tau accumulation could be assessed only in a small subset of patients
- Lack of diversity in the study population, as most participants were White and well educated
- Small sample size (15 to 28 patients per tilavonemab dose group) in the younger-than-65-years subgroup contributes to uncertainty about the observed treatment effect in that subgroup
- The magnitude of decrease in CSF tau necessary for clinical benefit is unknown

### Where authoritative sources disagree

- **Would higher doses of tilavonemab produce clinical benefit?** — This document argues higher doses are unlikely to help, because the CSF free tau reduction plateaus at higher exposures. *(Florian et al., citing the PSP study by Höglinger et al.)*
