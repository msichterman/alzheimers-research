## Takeaway

In May 2025, the FDA cleared the first blood test to aid in Alzheimer's disease diagnostics: the Fujirebio Lumipulse G pTau217/ß-Amyloid 1-42 Plasma Ratio. While highly accurate compared to PET and CSF benchmarks, it is not a standalone diagnostic and yields indeterminate results in roughly one-fifth of patients.

## Key findings

- Demonstrated 91.7% positive agreement and 97.3% negative agreement with PET or CSF amyloid tests in a 499-patient cohort (FDA press release).
- Reduces reliance on costly PET scans and invasive lumbar punctures by detecting the presence of brain amyloid plaques via a routine blood draw (FDA press release).

## Methods in brief

Retrospective multi-center clinical validation study analyzing 499 plasma samples from cognitively impaired adults aged 55 and older. Plasma ratio results were compared directly against established amyloid PET scans or CSF assays.

## Trial evidence

The FDA clearance is supported by a retrospective pooled validation of 499 samples drawn from observational cohorts and clinical trials, including [POLARIS-AD](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05531526), [Bio-Hermes](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04733989), and [BioFINDER-2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03174938). A peer-reviewed analysis of this dataset was [published in July 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42418465/), confirming a 91.8% positive predictive value and 19.6% indeterminate rate, effectively matching the FDA's reported figures.

## Public sentiment

Public and patient response is highly hopeful regarding lower out-of-pocket costs and reduced invasiveness, though online caregiver communities express frustration over insurance coverage hurdles and the anxiety of indeterminate results. The scientific community views the clearance as a major milestone but warns against off-label use due to age-related biomarker confounders in amyloid-negative individuals.

## Caveats

- Almost one in five patients (~20%) will receive an indeterminate result, requiring them to undergo traditional PET or CSF testing anyway.
- Clearance is strictly for symptomatic adults aged 55 and older; it is not authorized for asymptomatic screening and must be interpreted alongside other clinical evaluations.
- The summary data lacks demographic breakdowns, leaving it unclear if diagnostic accuracy holds across diverse populations and varying comorbidities.
