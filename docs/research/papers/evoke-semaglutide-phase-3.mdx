## Takeaway

Oral semaglutide (14 mg daily) failed to slow clinical progression in early Alzheimer's disease. The Phase 3 EVOKE and EVOKE+ trials were discontinued after demonstrating no significant difference from placebo on cognitive or functional decline after 104 weeks.

## Key findings

- Semaglutide showed no significant difference compared to placebo on the CDR-SB at week 104 (treatment difference -0.08, p=0.57) ([Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41865758/)).
- The clinical development program for semaglutide in Alzheimer's disease was completely discontinued due to the definitive negative outcome.

## Methods in brief

A randomized, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial enrolling 1,840 participants (aged 55-85) with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia of the Alzheimer's type and confirmed amyloid positivity. Participants received oral semaglutide (up to 14 mg once daily) or placebo for up to 173 weeks, with the primary endpoint assessing CDR-SB change at 104 weeks ([NCT04777396](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04777396)).

## Trial evidence

EVOKE ([NCT04777396](/trials/evoke-nct04777396)) and its sister trial EVOKE+ ([NCT04777409](/trials/evoke-plus-nct04777409)) definitively established that GLP-1 receptor agonists do not halt disease progression in symptomatic early Alzheimer's disease. This aligns with prior negative Phase 2 results from liraglutide (ELAD). While observational target trial emulations suggest GLP-1s might lower Alzheimer's risk before symptom onset, these randomized trials prove they are ineffective as a symptomatic treatment.

## Public sentiment

Public perception features a stark divide between clinical reality and patient hope. Investors and pharmaceutical media quickly digested the definitive trial failure, but misinformation continues to circulate among GLP-1 weight-loss communities, where some mistakenly believe semaglutide provides proven neuroprotection or memory improvement in humans.

## Caveats

- The EVOKE program tested semaglutide strictly as a disease-modifying treatment in symptomatic patients; these results do not rule out potential preventative benefits if the drug is initiated years before cognitive decline begins.
