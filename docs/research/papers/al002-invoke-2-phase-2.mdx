**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2025](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04592874) · [Nature Medicine, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04273-1)
**Authors**: Alector Inc. (Sponsor)

## Takeaway

AL002, a TREM2-activating monoclonal antibody, failed to slow cognitive or functional decline over 96 weeks in patients with early Alzheimer's disease. The drug also caused dose-dependent increases in amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), severely challenging the hypothesis that targeting microglia via TREM2 could provide a safer or more effective alternative to plaque-clearing antibodies.

## Key findings

- Failed to meet the primary endpoint of CDR-SB change at 96 weeks across all three doses (p>0.63 for all treatment arms vs. placebo) ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04592874)).
- Missed all secondary cognitive and functional endpoints, showing no statistically significant differences on MMSE, RBANS, ADAS-Cog13, ADCS-ADL-MCI, and ADCOMS ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04592874)).
- Significantly increased the risk of ARIA-E (21–23% across AL002 doses vs. 1.1% for placebo) and ARIA-H (27–29% across doses vs. 4.5% for placebo) ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04592874)).

## Methods in brief

Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study evaluating three doses of intravenous AL002 versus placebo. The trial enrolled 356 adults aged 50-85 with early Alzheimer's disease and confirmed brain amyloidosis for a 96-week treatment period ([NCT04592874](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04592874)).

## Trial evidence

The decisive failure of the INVOKE-2 trial led to the early termination of its open-label extension ([NCT05744401](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05744401)). These results contradict the safety and target engagement signals from the Phase 1 trial ([NCT03635047](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03635047)), marking a significant setback for the clinical development of AL002 and the broader TREM2 microglial hypothesis.

## Public sentiment

Public and scientific sentiment is highly negative following the readout. The trial failure caused a sharp stock decline and a 17% workforce reduction at Alector ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/alectors-abbvie-partnered-alzheimers-program-fails-phase-ii-triggering-a-17-layoff)). Researchers are now debating whether TREM2 is an invalid target or simply tested too late in the disease process, while patient communities remain mostly unaware of the drug, focusing instead on approved anti-amyloid therapies.

## Caveats

- APOE4 homozygotes were excluded from the trial, meaning the drug's safety profile (particularly ARIA risk) in the highest-risk genetic group remains unknown.
- This page reflects top-line registry data; biomarker outcomes (e.g., CSF or PET changes) that might explain the biological effect of the drug require consulting the peer-reviewed publication.
- There is a numerical discrepancy in participant counts between the registry (356 enrolled) and the subsequent journal publication (381 randomized).
