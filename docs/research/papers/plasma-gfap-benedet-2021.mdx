**Source**: [JAMA Neurology, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2021.3671) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34661615/)
**Authors**: Andréa L. Benedet et al.

## Takeaway

Plasma GFAP levels rise significantly during preclinical Alzheimer's disease and accurately discriminate amyloid-positive from amyloid-negative individuals. The magnitude of this change and its diagnostic accuracy consistently outperform cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) GFAP. The evidence is robust, drawing from three independent observational cohorts, positioning plasma GFAP as a highly sensitive, blood-based proxy for early amyloid-related astrogliosis.

## Key findings

- Plasma GFAP levels showed a 54% increase in preclinical AD and a 107% increase in AD dementia compared to amyloid-negative individuals in the TRIAD cohort (paper, Results).
- Plasma GFAP discriminated amyloid-positive from amyloid-negative individuals with higher accuracy (AUC 0.69–0.86) than CSF GFAP (AUC 0.59–0.76) across all cohorts (paper, Table 2).
- Plasma GFAP elevations were specifically driven by amyloid-β pathology, which mediated 60-63% of GFAP's association with tau, whereas CSF GFAP correlated more with broad neuroinflammation markers like sTREM2 (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Observational cross-sectional study of 871 individuals across the AD continuum. Participants were drawn from the TRIAD (Canada), ALFA+ (Spain), and BioCogBank (France) cohorts between July 2014 and January 2020.

## Trial evidence

The findings are grounded in three well-established independent cohorts. The [ALFA Cohort](/trials/alfa-cohort-nct02485730) prospectively tracks early preclinical biomarker profiles in cognitively healthy adults. Active recruitment in related cohorts like [BioCogBank-AD](/trials/biocogbank-ad-nct06582199) aims to further validate these blood-based markers for clinical utility in symptomatic stages.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is highly optimistic, though consumer enthusiasm often outpaces scientific reality. Mainstream media and retail investors frequently hype plasma GFAP as a definitive 'finger-prick' test that can predict dementia decades early. In contrast, researchers view GFAP as a valuable but specific proxy for amyloid-related astrogliosis that must be interpreted alongside other markers (like p-tau217) in multiplex diagnostic panels.

## Caveats

- The cross-sectional design establishes correlation but cannot definitively prove longitudinal tracking of disease progression within individual patients.
- Definitions of amyloid-β pathology and available data varied across the three contributing cohorts, complicating direct comparisons.
- The study did not include measurements of amyloid-β in blood to directly compare against GFAP.
