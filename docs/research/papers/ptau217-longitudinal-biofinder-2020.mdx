## Takeaway

Plasma p-tau217 increases over time in preclinical and prodromal Alzheimer's disease, while remaining stable in individuals without amyloid pathology. This longitudinal tracking correlates strongly with both cognitive decline and brain atrophy, establishing p-tau217 as a reliable, minimally invasive biomarker for monitoring early disease progression.

## Key findings

- Preclinical (Aβ+ cognitively unimpaired) and prodromal (Aβ+ MCI) patients showed accelerated p-tau217 increases compared to Aβ- controls (paper, Results).
- P-tau217 remained stable in Aβ- participants and MCI patients who did not progress to AD dementia (paper, Results).
- Longitudinal p-tau217 increases correlated significantly with worsening cognitive scores on both the MMSE and mPACC (paper, Figure 2).
- Increases in p-tau217 correlated with accelerated thinning of the temporal cortex and hippocampal volume loss, particularly in cognitively unimpaired Aβ+ individuals (paper, Figure 3).
- Plasma neurofilament light (NfL) showed a slight increase over time across all groups but exhibited no AD-specific trajectory (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Prospective cohort study analyzing a 250-patient subgroup of the Swedish BioFINDER study ([NCT01208675](/trials/biofinder-nct01208675)). The study evaluated 150 cognitively unimpaired individuals and 100 with mild cognitive impairment over a median of 4.3 years.

## Trial evidence

This paper provides pivotal longitudinal validation for plasma p-tau217, drawing on a subcohort of the 1,150-subject Swedish BioFINDER study. It builds on previous cross-sectional evidence to demonstrate that p-tau217 tracks dynamically with disease progression. This foundational observational data has been critical for integrating p-tau217 into therapeutic clinical trials and commercial diagnostic assays.

## Public sentiment

Public sentiment surrounding p-tau217 blood tests is highly optimistic but accompanied by practical concerns. Patient and caregiver communities often express anxiety about predicting a disease with limited treatment options and report confusion navigating commercial test scores (e.g., [Reddit discussions on testing](https://www.reddit.com/r/Alzheimers/comments/17wdz9l/our_amyloid_test_came_back_a_95_does_anyone_know/)). Conversely, science enthusiasts and biotech investors view scalable blood tests as a revolutionary advancement poised to replace painful lumbar punctures and expensive PET scans.

## Caveats

- Subgroup sizes were relatively small (e.g., n=49 for Aβ+ mild cognitive impairment), which limited the statistical power to detect structural correlations like temporal cortex thinning in that group.
- The cohort was restricted exclusively to the Swedish BioFINDER study, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings across more diverse geographical and ethnic populations.
