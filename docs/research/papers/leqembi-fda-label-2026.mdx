**Source**: [U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medwatch)
**Authors**: Eisai Inc. and Biogen

## Takeaway

Lecanemab is an anti-amyloid monoclonal antibody approved for mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia stage Alzheimer's. It clears brain amyloid and slowed clinical decline by 27% on the CDR-SB over 18 months. The January 2026 label update adds a weekly subcutaneous maintenance autoinjector, though it retains strict boxed warnings for ARIA and requires ApoE4 testing.

## Key findings

- Slowed clinical decline by 27% (-0.45 points) on the CDR-SB over 18 months compared to placebo (Section 14, Table 10).
- Reduced amyloid beta plaque to less than 30 Centiloids in 67% of patients by week 79 (Section 12.2).
- ARIA occurred in 21% of treated patients, with ApoE4 homozygotes facing a 45% incidence rate compared to 19% in heterozygotes and 13% in noncarriers (Section 5.1).

## Methods in brief

Information based on two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials: Study 1 (Phase 2, N=856) and Study 2 (Phase 3, N=1795). Patients had mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia with confirmed amyloid pathology.

## Trial evidence

Lecanemab's efficacy is established by the pivotal Phase 3 Clarity AD trial ([NCT03887455](/trials/lecanemab-clarity-ad)) and the Phase 2b Study 201 ([NCT01767311](/research/papers/lecanemab-phase-2b)). Both trials demonstrated robust amyloid clearance and modest cognitive benefit. A minor discrepancy exists between the FDA label stating 1,795 randomized patients for Study 2 and the registry's 1,906 total enrollment, which simply reflects the modified intention-to-treat analysis population. The subcutaneous dosing option was evaluated separately in a Phase 1 bioequivalence study ([NCT05533801](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05533801)) and open-label cohorts, rather than in the core 18-month placebo-controlled trials.

## Public sentiment

Public perception is divided. Patients and caregivers heavily favor the new subcutaneous IQLIK autoinjector for its convenience over bi-weekly IV infusions. However, clinicians continue to debate the clinical meaningfulness of a 0.45 CDR-SB benefit, and high-profile safety concerns regarding ARIA—particularly for APOE4 homozygotes—dominate scientific discussions. Misinformation also persists, with some public factions mistakenly believing the drug is a cure or that the subcutaneous option eliminates the need for intensive MRI monitoring.

## Caveats

- While the subcutaneous autoinjector offers convenience, its clinical efficacy relies on pharmacokinetic modeling and open-label extensions rather than long-term, placebo-controlled data.
- Although lecanemab clears amyloid in ApoE4 homozygotes, exploratory analyses showed no significant clinical benefit on the primary endpoint for this highest-risk subgroup.
- Use of antithrombotic or thrombolytic medications increases the risk of severe intracerebral hemorrhage.
