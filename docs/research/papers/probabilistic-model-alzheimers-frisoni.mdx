**Authors**: Frisoni et al.

## Takeaway

The authors argue that the deterministic 'amyloid cascade' model fails to explain the full clinical spectrum of sporadic Alzheimer's disease, given the modest cognitive benefits of anti-amyloid therapies. They propose a probabilistic model that categorizes AD into three variants—autosomal dominant, APOE ε4-related, and APOE ε4-unrelated—where decreasing amyloid penetrance is offset by increasing influence from stochastic genetic and environmental factors.

## Key findings

- Anti-amyloid therapies clear plaques but yield only modest or inconsistent cognitive benefits, implying that over 50% of clinical progression is independent of Aβ deposition (paper, Evidence against).
- In autopsy series, the combination of isolated amyloidosis and tauopathy severe enough to justify dementia without other co-morbid pathologies accounts for less than 20% of cases (paper, Evidence against).
- The APOE ε4 genotype significantly modifies AD risk and presentation, with lifetime dementia risk reaching up to 95% for ε4/ε4 carriers compared to 20% for ε2/ε2 carriers (paper, APOE ε4-related sporadic AD).
- APOE ε4-unrelated sporadic AD accounts for 30–40% of amyloid-positive cases, typically presenting later in life and demonstrating heavy modulation by non-APOE genetics and lifestyle factors (paper, APOE ε4-unrelated sporadic AD).

## Methods in brief

This perspective article synthesizes epidemiological, clinical, and neuropathological evidence to propose a new conceptual model for Alzheimer's disease. It draws heavily on results from major anti-amyloid clinical trials and genome-wide association studies.

## Trial evidence

The authors base their critique of the deterministic amyloid hypothesis heavily on the mixed results of major anti-amyloid trials. They note that [gantenerumab and solanezumab](/trials/dian-tu-001) in autosomal dominant AD, as well as aducanumab ([EMERGE](/trials/aducanumab-emerge) and [ENGAGE](/trials/aducanumab-engage)), failed to halt or profoundly slow cognitive decline despite robust amyloid clearance. While the paper cites a 47% reduction in cognitive decline from lecanemab's Phase 2b [Study 201](/trials/lecanemab-study-201), the fully powered Phase 3 Clarity AD trial later demonstrated a 27% reduction, which further reinforces the authors' premise that the majority of disease progression occurs independently of Aβ.

## Public sentiment

Public and professional sentiment regarding the amyloid hypothesis remains highly fractured. Patient communities express desperate hope for anti-amyloid treatments while remaining anxious about side effects like ARIA. In contrast, the medical and scientific communities share the paper's skepticism toward the deterministic amyloid model, frequently debating the clinical meaningfulness of these drugs and heavily criticizing the FDA's controversial handling of aducanumab.

## Caveats

- The concepts of 'resistance' and 'resilience' used in the probabilistic model are theoretical constructs that currently elude direct biological observation.
- The proposed taxonomy relies heavily on APOE stratification but lacks extensive clinicopathological data on rarer, highly penetrant risk genes (like TREM2) that might identify other distinct AD subtypes.
