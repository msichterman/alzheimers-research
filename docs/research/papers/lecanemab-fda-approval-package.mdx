**Authors**: Buracchio, T. J., et al. — Office of Neuroscience, FDA

## Takeaway

The FDA granted traditional approval to lecanemab based on the Phase 3 CLARITY-AD trial, which demonstrated a 27% slowing of clinical decline over 18 months. However, the approval carries a Boxed Warning for ARIA and intracerebral hemorrhage, emphasizing that ApoE ε4 homozygotes face substantially higher risks.

## Key findings

- Lecanemab reduced clinical decline by 27% on the CDR-SB compared to placebo at 18 months (treatment difference -0.451; p=0.00005) (FDA Review, Table 6).
- Brain amyloid plaque levels decreased significantly, dropping by 59.1 Centiloids relative to placebo at week 79 (FDA Review, Table 11).
- ARIA-E occurred in 33% of ApoE ε4 homozygotes on lecanemab, compared to 11% of heterozygotes and 5% of noncarriers (FDA Review, Table 21).
- Intracerebral hemorrhage occurred in 2.5% of patients taking lecanemab with an anticoagulant, compared to 0% of those on placebo (FDA Review, Table 29).

## Methods in brief

Traditional approval was based primarily on Study 301 (CLARITY-AD, NCT03887455), an 18-month Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The FDA evaluated 1,795 patients aged 50 to 90 with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to Alzheimer’s disease and confirmed amyloid pathology.

## Trial evidence

Lecanemab's traditional approval is anchored by the Phase 3 CLARITY-AD trial ([NCT03887455](/trials/clarity-ad-nct03887455)), which met its primary clinical endpoint and corroborated biomarker findings from the earlier Study 201 ([NCT01767311](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01767311)). The FDA reviewed the 1,795 randomized participants from CLARITY-AD, noting robust efficacy but delineating severe safety risks concentrated in ApoE ε4 homozygotes and patients on anticoagulants.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of lecanemab is highly divided. Caregivers express hope and a willingness to accept severe side effects, while the medical community voices frustration over the heavy diagnostic and MRI monitoring burdens required (Reddit). The scientific consensus remains highly skeptical of the drug's use in ApoE ε4 homozygotes due to high hemorrhage risks and minimal observed clinical benefit, frequently correcting the public misconception that the drug is a cure (Alzforum).

## Caveats

- Exploratory analyses showed no clinical benefit on the primary endpoint for ApoE ε4 homozygotes, despite this group bearing the highest risk for severe ARIA and intracerebral hemorrhage (FDA Review, Section 1).
- The FDA statistical reviewer challenged the sponsor's claim that lecanemab preserves 5.3 months of time, noting a quadratic model fits the data better than a linear one (FDA Review, Section 6).
- Fatal intracerebral hemorrhages occurred in the open-label extension, particularly in patients with underlying cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) or concurrent use of anticoagulants (FDA Review, Section 7).
- Black/African American patients were under-represented in the clinical trials compared to the general U.S. Alzheimer's population (FDA Review, Section 1).

### Funding & conflicts

Study funded by Eisai Inc. and Biogen. The applicant adequately disclosed financial interests or agreements with clinical investigators; 13 investigators in Japan had disclosable financial interests but enrolled low numbers of patients, mitigating bias risk.

### Bias & spin flags

- Exploratory subgroup analyses showed no clinical benefit on the primary endpoint (CDR-SB) for ApoE ε4 homozygotes, who also bore the highest risk for severe ARIA and intracerebral hemorrhages.
- The sponsor's claim that lecanemab preserves 5.3 months of time relative to placebo was challenged by the statistical reviewer because the assumption of linearity over time was questionable (a quadratic model fit better).
- Several fatal intracerebral hemorrhages occurred in the open-label extension, specifically in patients with underlying cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) or concurrent use of anticoagulants/thrombolytics (e.g., tPA).

### Cross-examination

2 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Fact check (corrected)** — Study 301 enrolled 1,906 participants. ClinicalTrials.gov lists 1,906 enrolled, but the FDA review is based on the 1,795 randomized participants. We use 1,795 for the final content.
- **Fact check (disputed)** — Lecanemab preserves 5.3 months of time relative to placebo. The FDA statistical reviewer challenged the assumption of linearity over time, stating a quadratic model fits the data better than a linear one.
- **Registry cross-check** — The FDA document reports the Study 301 (CLARITY-AD) enrollment as 1,795 participants (which refers to the final randomized cohort), whereas ClinicalTrials.gov reports the actual enrollment count as 1,906 participants.
- **Misinformation watch** — Leqembi is a cure for Alzheimer's disease (it only slows progression)
- **Misinformation watch** — Leqembi can be used for moderate or advanced/late-stage Alzheimer's (it is only indicated for early symptomatic stages)
- **Misinformation watch** — ApoE4 homozygotes experience the same clinical benefits as other patients despite the higher risks
