**Source**: [JAMA, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.12134) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32722745)
**Authors**: Palmqvist et al.

## Takeaway

Plasma P-tau217 is a highly accurate blood-based diagnostic tool for Alzheimer's disease. It performs comparably to established CSF and PET measures, clearly distinguishing Alzheimer's from other neurodegenerative disorders, and can detect pathological changes up to 20 years before symptom onset in genetic forms of the disease.

## Key findings

- Plasma P-tau217 differentiated neuropathologically defined Alzheimer's from non-Alzheimer's with an AUC of 0.89, significantly outperforming plasma P-tau181 and NfL (paper, Results).
- In a clinical cohort, the biomarker discriminated Alzheimer's dementia from other neurodegenerative diseases with 96% accuracy (AUC 0.96), performing comparably to CSF and PET measures (paper, Results).
- Among PSEN1 E280A familial mutation carriers, P-tau217 levels were significantly elevated starting around age 25, approximately 20 years before the estimated onset of mild cognitive impairment (paper, Results).
- Plasma P-tau217 correlated with tau tangles only in individuals who already had β-amyloid plaques (paper, Results).

## Methods in brief

Cross-sectional study evaluating 1,402 participants across three diverse cohorts: an Arizona neuropathology cohort (n=81), the Swedish BioFINDER-2 clinical cohort (n=699), and a Colombian autosomal-dominant Alzheimer's kindred (n=622).

## Trial evidence

This study draws foundational cross-sectional validation data from the ongoing [BioFINDER 2](/trials/biofinder-2-nct03174938) cohort. To analyze predictive timelines in genetic early-onset disease, it utilizes the broader 622-person observational registry supporting the [API ADAD](/trials/api-adad-nct01998841) therapeutic trial.

## Public sentiment

Public and media reception of the P-tau217 blood test is highly enthusiastic, framing it as a revolutionary, accessible alternative to expensive PET scans and invasive lumbar punctures. However, mainstream media frequently oversimplifies the findings, erroneously reporting that the test acts as a universal [20-year predictive clock](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/blood-test-predicts-start-alzheimers-disease-symptoms) for the general public.

## Caveats

- The 20-year prediction timeline observed in this study applies specifically to individuals with an autosomal-dominant genetic mutation; it cannot be used as a precise symptom clock for the broader population with sporadic Alzheimer's.
- As a cross-sectional study, it evaluates different individuals at various disease stages rather than tracking intra-individual biomarker changes over time.

### Cross-examination

4 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Registry cross-check** — The document mentions evaluating a Colombian autosomal-dominant AD kindred (PSEN1 E280A) cohort of 622 participants. While this group belongs to the Alzheimer's Prevention Initiative (API), the formal API ADAD therapeutic trial (NCT01998841) for this specific mutation only enrolled 252 participants; the 2020 biomarker paper draws from the broader observational family registry rather than exclusively from the clinical trial subjects.
- **Misinformation watch** — The P-tau217 blood test acts as a definitive standalone 'clock' that can predict the exact year an individual with sporadic Alzheimer's will develop clinical symptoms.
- **Misinformation watch** — A positive P-tau217 blood test alone is sufficient for a clinical diagnosis, replacing the need for cognitive assessments or physician evaluations.
