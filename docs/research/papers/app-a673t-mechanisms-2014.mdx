**Source**: [The Journal of Biological Chemistry, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.589069) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25253696)
**Authors**: Maloney et al. — Genentech, Inc.

## Takeaway

The A673T (Icelandic) mutation in the APP gene offers lifelong protection against Alzheimer's disease by inherently reducing BACE1 cleavage and decreasing amyloid-beta aggregation. However, mimicking this mechanism with acute BACE1 inhibitor drugs in older adults has consistently failed in the clinic.

## Key findings

- The A673T mutation decreases the catalytic turnover rate (Vmax) of APP by BACE1 without altering the enzyme's substrate affinity (Km) (Abstract).
- The mutation specifically reduces aggregation of Aβ(1-42) peptides, a trend that persists even in physiologically relevant 1:9 mixtures with Aβ(1-40) (Abstract).
- Reduced APP cleavage by BACE1 was confirmed in both mouse primary neurons and human iPSC-derived neurons (Abstract).

## Methods in brief

In vitro biochemical assays and cell-based models (transfected mouse primary neurons and isogenic human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons).

## Trial evidence

While this genetic discovery provided a strong biological rationale for targeting BACE1, pharmacological translation has completely failed. Every major BACE1 inhibitor tested in Phase 2/3 trials—including [verubecestat](/research/papers/verubecestat-epoch)—was halted prematurely. Acute pharmacological inhibition of BACE1 in older adults frequently caused paradoxical cognitive worsening and toxicity, revealing a stark contrast between lifelong, modest genetic protection and late-life drug intervention.

## Public sentiment

Public discussion of the A673T mutation is mostly confined to academic and science media, where it is viewed as foundational proof of the amyloid cascade hypothesis. Patient and investor communities remain quiet on the topic due to the lack of an actionable therapeutic path, a sentiment strongly reinforced by the historical failures of the BACE1 inhibitor drug class.

## Caveats

- The study relies entirely on biochemical and cell culture models, which cannot capture off-target effects or toxicities that occur in a whole organism.
- The A673T mutation confers a subtle, lifelong reduction in amyloid processing, which fundamentally differs from the acute, high-dose inhibition achieved by clinical drugs.
