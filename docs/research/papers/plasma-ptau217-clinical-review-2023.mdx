**Authors**: Hansson et al. — Skane University Hospital / University of Gothenburg

## Takeaway

Blood-based biomarkers like plasma p-tau217 can accurately distinguish Alzheimer's from other neurodegenerative diseases. They are already transforming clinical trials by replacing expensive PET screening and are expected to shift routine diagnosis into primary care once analytical standardization is achieved.

## Key findings

- Plasma p-tau217 shows a 300–700% increase in symptomatic Alzheimer's patients compared to healthy individuals, making it a highly robust marker (paper).
- The plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio only drops 8–15% in symptomatic disease, resulting in poor robustness for routine clinical use (paper).
- Prescreening with blood biomarkers drastically improves trial efficiency; for context, the historical A4 trial required over 4,000 PET scans across 3.5 years just to enroll 1,169 participants (paper).

## Methods in brief

Narrative review synthesizing evidence on the diagnostic performance, robustness, and clinical implementation of blood-based biomarkers for Alzheimer's disease.

## Trial evidence

This review reflects a major clinical shift: blood biomarkers are replacing PET scans for prescreening in trials like [AHEAD 3-45](/trials/ahead-3-45). The authors argue for the eventual use of these markers in primary care, a claim they are prospectively testing in the ongoing [BioFINDER - Primary Care](/trials/biofinder-pc-nct06120361) trial.

## Public sentiment

Public and investor sentiment is highly optimistic, focusing on the avoidance of invasive spinal taps and expensive PET scans. However, the media often mischaracterizes the tests as standalone diagnostics for asymptomatic individuals, leading to distress among patients who test positive without available preventative treatments.

## Caveats

- The authors hold significant financial conflicts of interest, including patent rights to p-tau217 assays and ownership in biomarker startups.
- Current diagnostic cutoffs are predominantly based on retrospective, batch-analyzed data rather than real-world prospective testing.
