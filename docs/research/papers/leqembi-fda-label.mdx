**Source**: [U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2023](https://www.fda.gov/drugsatfda)
**Authors**: Eisai Inc. and Biogen

## Takeaway

Lecanemab (Leqembi) is FDA-approved for mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia stages of Alzheimer's disease, showing a 27% slowing of clinical decline over 18 months. However, it carries a boxed warning for ARIA (brain swelling and bleeding), which is significantly more frequent and severe in ApoE ε4 homozygotes—a subgroup that also showed no primary clinical benefit in exploratory analyses.

## Key findings

- Lecanemab reduced clinical decline on the CDR-SB by 27% (-0.45 difference) at 18 months (p&lt;0.0001) in Study 2 (CLARITY AD).
- The drug reduces amyloid beta plaque levels dose- and time-dependently, with Study 2 showing continuous reductions through 18 months.
- ApoE ε4 homozygotes had a 45% incidence of ARIA compared to 19% in heterozygotes and 13% in noncarriers, with severe ARIA-H reaching 13.5%.
- An exploratory analysis found no treatment effect on the primary clinical endpoint (CDR-SB) for ApoE ε4 homozygotes.
- Patients taking lecanemab concomitantly with anticoagulants experienced a 2.5% incidence of intracerebral hemorrhage, compared to 0% on placebo.

## Methods in brief

Double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, randomized studies evaluating 2,651 patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia associated with Alzheimer's disease and confirmed amyloid pathology. Treatment duration spanned up to 18 months across two pivotal trials (NCT01767311 and NCT03887455).

## Trial evidence

Efficacy is supported primarily by [Study 2 (CLARITY AD)](/trials/clarity-ad-nct03887455), which met its primary endpoint for reducing clinical decline, and Study 1, a Phase 2 trial that demonstrated significant amyloid clearance. While Study 1 is cited in the label for its biomarker success, the label notes it missed its 12-month primary clinical composite endpoint (Week 53).

## Public sentiment

Public reception is highly stratified: retail investors and pharmaceutical media celebrate the drug's commercial expansion, while patients and caregivers express deep anxiety over brain bleeding risks and logistical burdens. A significant disconnect persists between the clinical reality of modestly slowed decline and media narratives falsely implying cognitive restoration.

## Caveats

- ApoE ε4 homozygotes (about 15% of patients) face double the ARIA risk and drastically higher rates of severe microhemorrhages, yet exploratory analyses showed no primary clinical benefit for this subgroup.
- Using anticoagulants alongside Leqembi significantly elevates the risk of intracerebral hemorrhage.
- The incidence of neutralizing anti-drug antibodies might be underestimated due to assay interference from serum lecanemab concentrations.
- Despite public relations narratives of 'improvement,' the drug's proven clinical benefit is a moderate slowing of inevitable cognitive decline, not disease reversal.
