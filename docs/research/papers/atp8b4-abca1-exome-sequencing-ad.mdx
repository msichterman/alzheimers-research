## Takeaway

Rare, damaging genetic variants in *ATP8B4* and *ABCA1* significantly increase the risk of Alzheimer's disease. Variants with the largest effect sizes, primarily loss-of-function mutations, are strongly enriched in early-onset cases, reinforcing the critical role of lipid metabolism and microglial function in disease pathology.

## Key findings

- Damaging variants in *ATP8B4* increase Alzheimer's risk (OR 1.5), as do variants in *ABCA1* (OR 1.7) (Table 1).
- Loss-of-function variants in *SORL1*, *ADAM10*, *CLU*, and *ZCWPW1* are associated with the earliest median age of onset, with *SORL1* carriers showing a median onset of 62 years (Table 3).
- Rare-variant burdens in *RIN3*, *CLU*, *ZCWPW1*, and *ACE* act independently of common GWAS sentinel variants, indicating these are likely the functional driver genes at those loci (Table 2).

## Methods in brief

Multistage gene burden analysis using exome sequencing data from 32,558 individuals (16,036 Alzheimer's cases, including 4,060 early-onset, and 16,522 controls), primarily of European ancestry.

## Trial evidence

This foundational genetics research validates microglial function and lipid metabolism as key disease drivers, particularly through genes like *TREM2*. However, translating these genetic insights into therapies has proven difficult. The leading clinical candidate targeting *TREM2*, the agonistic antibody AL002, failed to show clinical or functional benefit in the [INVOKE-2](/trials/invoke-2-nct04592874) Phase 2 trial for early AD, highlighting a profound discrepancy between genetic risk association and clinical therapeutic tractability.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of these findings is largely limited to the scientific community, where the study is celebrated as robust validation of the lipid metabolism and microglial hypotheses. Patients, caregivers, and retail investors remain quiet on the topic, appropriately recognizing these findings as long-term foundational target discoveries rather than actionable near-term therapeutics.

## Caveats

- Because *APOE* status was a selection criterion for many underlying cohorts, the primary burden tests could not straightforwardly isolate the effect of these rare variants from *APOE4* dosage.
- The study is underpowered for extremely rare variants (such as those in *ADAM10*), meaning some true genetic risk factors likely failed to reach exome-wide significance.
- By focusing exclusively on coding variants in exome data, the impact of structural, copy number, and non-coding regulatory variations remains unknown.
