**Source**: [Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13195-023-01169-x) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36849969)
**Authors**: Dubois et al. — AB Science

## Takeaway

Masitinib at 4.5 mg/kg/day demonstrated a statistically significant slowing of cognitive decline on the ADAS-Cog in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. However, the trial's evidence is highly uncertain due to a late protocol amendment lowering the bar for success, severe patient dropout rates in the active arm, and the failure of a higher 6.0 mg/kg/day dose. A confirmatory Phase 3 trial is underway to address these methodological weaknesses.

## Key findings

- Patients receiving 4.5 mg/kg/day masitinib showed significantly less cognitive decline than placebo on the ADAS-Cog (-2.15 point difference, p&lt;0.001) (paper, Table 2).
- The 4.5 mg/kg/day arm missed the required 2.5% alpha significance level for functional improvement on the ADCS-ADL (1.82 point difference, p=0.038) (paper, Table 2).
- A separate, titrated 6.0 mg/kg/day dose arm failed to show significant benefit on either cognitive or functional measures (paper, Table 2).

## Methods in brief

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial (NCT01872598) spanning 24 weeks. The trial enrolled 718 patients aged 50 and older with mild-to-moderate probable Alzheimer's disease (MMSE 12–25) treated with standard-of-care cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine.

## Trial evidence

This document reports the principal findings of the Phase 3 [AB09004](/trials/masitinib-ab09004) trial. Due to shifting endpoints and a lack of biomarker confirmation, the clinical evidence for masitinib remains weak. The sponsor has initiated a confirmatory Phase 3 trial ([AB21004](/trials/masitinib-ab21004)) restricted to mild Alzheimer's patients with confirmed amyloid/tau biomarkers to substantiate the drug's efficacy.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of masitinib is highly polarized. Retail investors and biotech enthusiasts frequently hype the drug on social media as a broad-spectrum treatment that halts cognitive decline with minimal side effects. In contrast, the scientific community and informed patient groups note its high dropout rates, lack of biomarker data, and past regulatory struggles in Europe for other indications.

## Caveats

- Efficacy was declared based solely on the ADAS-Cog cognitive scale; the drug failed to produce a statistically significant functional benefit on the ADCS-ADL.
- Changing the statistical bar for success late in the trial (after 81% of patients reached 24 weeks) significantly weakens the reliability of the positive result.
- Trial dropouts were substantially higher in the active treatment arm (34.5% vs 14.6%), meaning the positive results heavily rely on statistical imputation for missing patient data.
- The trial enrolled patients based on clinical symptoms without confirming amyloid or tau pathology, raising the possibility that some participants did not actually have Alzheimer's disease.

### Cross-examination

4 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Fact check (disputed)** — Total trial enrollment of 718 participants. The paper explicitly calculates 718 randomized patients across all arms, while the clinical registry records 721. Carried 718 forward as the analyzed population.
- **Registry cross-check** — The registry description for NCT01872598 explicitly defines ADCS-ADL and ADAS-Cog as 'co-primary' outcome measures (requiring both to be met for full success), whereas the document reveals a late protocol amendment split them into multiple independent primary endpoints, allowing the trial to claim success based solely on the ADAS-Cog result.
- **Registry cross-check** — The document states a final trial enrollment of 718 participants, but the registry records 721.
- **Misinformation watch** — Claims on Reddit that masitinib has 'almost no negative effects', which ignores the high 34.5% premature discontinuation rate and documented gastrointestinal/edema adverse events.
- **Misinformation watch** — Framing the AB09004 trial as an unqualified success by solely focusing on the 4.5 mg/kg/day arm while omitting the failure of the higher 6.0 mg/kg/day dose arm.
