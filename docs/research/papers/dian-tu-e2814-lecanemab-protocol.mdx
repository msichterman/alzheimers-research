**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2022](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05269394)
**Authors**: Washington University School of Medicine

## Takeaway

The DIAN-TU platform trial is testing a combination therapy strategy—the anti-MTBR tau antibody E2814 alongside the anti-amyloid drug lecanemab. The study hypothesizes that this dual approach may prevent or slow neurodegeneration in individuals with dominantly inherited Alzheimer's disease (DIAD), though no clinical efficacy results are yet available.

## Key findings

- This is an active registry record; no trial results or cognitive efficacy data have been reported (source document).
- Symptomatic participants receive lecanemab immediately, with E2814 or placebo added at week 24 in a blinded fashion.
- Asymptomatic participants start E2814 or placebo immediately, with open-label lecanemab added at week 52.
- The primary endpoint measures tau spread via tau PET at weeks 104 and 208 in symptomatic participants.

## Methods in brief

Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled platform trial. Enrolled 197 individuals (aged 18-80) with a known Alzheimer's disease-causing genetic mutation who are within 10 years of their predicted or actual cognitive symptom onset. The trial duration is up to 208 weeks (registered as NCT05269394).

## Trial evidence

This record details the active E2814/lecanemab arm of the DIAN-TU platform ([NCT05269394](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05269394)). Prior cohorts in the master protocol (NCT01760005) testing gantenerumab and solanezumab failed to meet their primary cognitive endpoints. Phase 1b data for E2814 (NCT04971733) indicated preliminary target engagement and tolerability, supporting its advancement into this combination efficacy trial.

## Public sentiment

Public perception among patients and families affected by DIAD is highly hopeful, with many viewing the combined amyloid and tau approach as a logical defense against the disease. However, there is a tendency in patient communities to prematurely interpret theoretical mechanisms or early biomarker signals as guaranteed clinical protection.

## Caveats

- The source document is purely a registry record providing study design, without any clinical efficacy or safety data.
- The hypothesized benefit of combining anti-amyloid and anti-tau therapies remains theoretical until Phase 3 cognitive outcomes are published.
