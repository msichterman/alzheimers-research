## Takeaway

The rare TREM2 R47H missense mutation nearly triples the risk of late-onset Alzheimer's disease, conferring a risk level comparable to the ApoE ε4 allele. This foundational 2013 genetic discovery established microglial inflammation as a core disease pathway and sparked the development of TREM2-targeted therapies.

## Key findings

- The TREM2 R47H mutation (rs75932628-T) increases Alzheimer's risk with a combined odds ratio of 2.90 across international cohorts (paper, RESULTS).
- The risk effect is comparable to the ApoE ε4 allele, which showed an odds ratio of 3.08 in the same comparison group of elderly controls (paper, RESULTS).
- Mutation carriers had an average age of onset 3.18 years earlier than non-carriers in the Icelandic cohort (paper, RESULTS).
- Cognitively intact elderly carriers between 80 and 100 years old showed poorer cognitive function than non-carriers (paper, RESULTS).

## Methods in brief

Case-control genetic association study. Researchers sequenced 2,261 Icelanders, identified functional variants, and imputed them into 3,550 Alzheimer's cases and over 111,000 controls. Findings were replicated in cohorts from the US, Norway, the Netherlands, and Germany.

## Trial evidence

The discovery of the TREM2 variant generated the hypothesis that pharmacological activation of TREM2 could clear amyloid plaques. However, clinical translation has proven difficult. Alector's TREM2 agonistic antibody (AL002) failed its Phase 2 [INVOKE-2](/trials/invoke-2-al002) trial in 2024, showing no clinical benefit on the CDR-SB over placebo and causing ARIA-like MRI changes. Despite this setback for antibodies, the industry has pivoted toward small-molecule TREM2 agonists like Vigil Neuroscience's VG-3927, which have recently completed Phase 1 safety testing to determine if better brain penetration can overcome the mechanism's initial clinical failures.

## Public sentiment

Public and investor sentiment regarding TREM2 therapies is highly polarized. Initial excitement cooled significantly following the high-profile failure of AL002, leading to skepticism among researchers about antibody-based microglial modulation. However, investor enthusiasm has recently pivoted to small molecules following [Sanofi's August 2026 buyout](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/sanofi-makes-alzheimers-play-vigil-takeover) of Vigil Neuroscience, shifting the narrative from a failed target to a failed delivery mechanism. The patient community remains largely quiet and fatigued by Phase 2 failures.

## Caveats

- The TREM2 R47H variant is extremely rare (0.46% frequency in elderly controls), meaning it accounts for only a tiny fraction of overall Alzheimer's cases despite its high individual risk.
- Researchers found no distinct clinical features—such as unique radiographic signs or differing rates of disease progression—to distinguish TREM2 mutation carriers from non-carriers.
- The variant's effect on lowering the age of disease onset was not statistically significant in the isolated Icelandic cohort due to the variant's rarity and resulting high standard error.
