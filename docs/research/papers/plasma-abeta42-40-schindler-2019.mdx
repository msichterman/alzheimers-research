## Takeaway

Plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 measured via high-precision mass spectrometry strongly predicts amyloid PET status, especially when combined with age and APOE4 status. Cognitively normal individuals with a negative PET scan but a positive blood test have a 15-fold higher risk of turning PET-positive in the future.

## Key findings

- Plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 correlated strongly with amyloid PET status (AUC 0.88) and CSF p-tau181/Aβ42 (AUC 0.85) (Neurology).
- Combining the plasma assay with age and APOE ε4 status yielded an AUC of 0.94 for predicting amyloid PET status (Neurology).
- Participants with a negative baseline amyloid PET scan but a positive plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio (&lt;0.1218) showed a 15-fold greater risk of converting to PET-positive (p=0.01) (Neurology).

## Methods in brief

Observational cohort study (Class II evidence) analyzing plasma and CSF samples from 158 mostly cognitively normal individuals, collected within 18 months of an amyloid PET scan.

## Trial evidence

This foundational study led directly to the commercial PrecivityAD test. The assay's accuracy was prospectively validated in the community-based [SEABIRD trial](/trials/seabird-nct03899844). Its real-world clinical utility was subsequently established in the [QUIP I](/trials/quip-i-nct05477056) study, which demonstrated that access to these blood biomarker results significantly alters physician diagnostic decision-making and patient management.

## Public sentiment

The medical and patient communities view plasma amyloid tests highly optimistically as accessible, minimally invasive alternatives to spinal taps and PET scans. However, hype outpaces evidence in the general public, where patients often incorrectly assume a positive amyloid blood test equates to a definitive and immediate diagnosis of clinical Alzheimer's dementia.

## Caveats

- The 15-fold increased risk for PET conversion relies on a longitudinal subset of initially PET-negative participants, suggesting a small sample size for that specific finding.
- The assay measures the probability of amyloid pathology, not clinical dementia; a positive result in a cognitively normal individual does not guarantee symptom progression.
