## Takeaway

In early 2026, the FDA issued a Class 2 device recall for the Lumipulse G pTau217/Aβ1-42 plasma ratio test. Post-market use revealed the test yielded an unexpectedly high frequency of false-positive and indeterminate results, challenging the high diagnostic specificity initially observed in clinical trials.

## Key findings

- The test produced falsely elevated positive and indeterminate ratios when compared back to FDA-cleared CSF testing and PET imaging (FDA recall notice).
- This defect risks incorrectly diagnosing patients with Alzheimer's-associated amyloid pathology or prompting unnecessary additional clinical testing (FDA recall notice).
- The recall impacts 128 units distributed across multiple U.S. states (FDA recall notice).

## Methods in brief

This is a Class 2 Device Recall notification posted by the FDA in February 2026. The manufacturer, Fujirebio Diagnostics, Inc., initiated the recall in December 2025 after observing inaccurate real-world results from distributed units.

## Trial evidence

Initial multicenter validation studies demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy for this plasma ratio. However, the FDA recall highlights a significant discrepancy, showing that the assay's real-world deployment suffered from low specificity and false-positive results that directly contradict the pristine performance seen in controlled clinical settings.

## Public sentiment

Public sentiment shifted from early enthusiasm for an accessible blood test to cautious skepticism following the recall. Scientific and medical commentary frames the event as an early warning about the premature clinical adoption of plasma biomarkers. Meanwhile, patient and caregiver communities remain largely quiet, suggesting the recall's specifics have not yet widely penetrated general consumer discussions.

## Caveats

- The notice is a regulatory action rather than a formal statistical analysis, meaning it does not provide exact real-world false-positive rates or confidence intervals.
- The technical cause of the assay's failure remains under investigation by the manufacturer, leaving it unclear if the issue is a localized manufacturing defect or a broader systemic flaw in the assay's design.
