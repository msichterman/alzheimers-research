**Source**: [ClinicalTrials.gov, 2025](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07169578)
**Authors**: Hoffmann-La Roche

## Takeaway

Trontinemab is entering Phase 3 testing following highly promising Phase 1/2 biomarker data. Its transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) 'brain shuttle' design aims to cross the blood-brain barrier more efficiently, rapidly clearing amyloid plaques while maintaining an exceptionally low rate of ARIA.

## Key findings

- The trial aims to enroll 800 participants to measure clinical efficacy via the CDR-SB at Week 72 (Source document).
- Prior Phase 1/2 data (Brainshuttle AD) demonstrated over 90% amyloid negativity by 6 months at 3.6 mg/kg, alongside normalization of downstream tau biomarkers (Alzforum).
- Phase 1/2 testing showed an ARIA-E rate of approximately 1%, suggesting the brain-shuttle mechanism successfully enhances target engagement while minimizing vascular toxicity (Alzforum).

## Methods in brief

This Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will enroll approximately 800 participants aged 50 to 90 with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (MCI to mild dementia). Participants must have amyloid pathology confirmed by PET or CSF. The primary treatment period spans 72 weeks with intravenous dosing.

## Trial evidence

TRONTIER 1 ([NCT07169578](/trials/trontinemab-nct07169578)) and its sister trial TRONTIER 2 are the pivotal Phase 3 efficacy studies for trontinemab. They build upon the Phase 1/2 [Brainshuttle AD](/trials/brainshuttle-ad-nct04639050) trial, which validated the TfR1 transport mechanism by demonstrating rapid amyloid clearance and normalization of downstream tau biomarkers. Note that while trontinemab lowers downstream tau, it is an amyloid-targeting drug, resolving a discrepancy in early database tagging.

## Public sentiment

Public and scientific sentiment is highly enthusiastic, driven by the drug's novel transport mechanism and the prospect of rapid plaque clearance with lower ARIA risk than first-generation therapies like lecanemab and donanemab. However, patient communities note the ongoing burden of intravenous infusions, and experts caution that the promising biomarker changes must still translate to definitive cognitive benefits in Phase 3.

## Caveats

- The Phase 3 study is currently recruiting; clinical efficacy on cognitive endpoints has not yet been demonstrated.
- Long-term safety, particularly monitoring for anemia observed in earlier trials, requires confirmation in this larger cohort.
- The registry record contains a minor typo in the inclusion criteria, requiring an RBANS DMI score of '85 or order' rather than '85 or under'.
