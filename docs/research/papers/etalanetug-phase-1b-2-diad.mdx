## Takeaway

Etalanetug demonstrated strong target engagement by substantially reducing CSF tau biomarkers in a very small study. However, severe adverse events emerged at higher doses, underscoring the need for safety validation in larger ongoing trials.

## Key findings

- Substantial reductions in CSF tau biomarkers at Day 757, including MTBR-tau243 (-381), p-tau181 (-606.64), and p-tau217 (-422.60) ([registry data](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04971733)).
- Serious adverse events occurred at higher dose levels, including cerebral venous sinus thrombosis and seizure in the 3000 mg group ([registry data](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04971733)).

## Methods in brief

Open-label Phase 1b/2 trial enrolling 8 adults with mild to moderate cognitive impairment due to dominantly inherited Alzheimer's disease (NCT04971733). Participants received intravenous etalanetug with intra-patient dose escalation up to 4500 mg over 120 weeks.

## Trial evidence

This Phase 1b/2 trial ([NCT04971733](/trials/e2814-nct04971733)) provides early biomarker validation following a Phase 1 healthy volunteer study ([NCT04231513](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04231513)). Because of the extremely small sample size and lack of placebo control, clinical efficacy cannot be established here. Etalanetug is currently being evaluated in larger controlled trials, including the DIAN-TU Phase 2/3 platform trial ([NCT05269394](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05269394)) and a Phase 2 study in early Alzheimer's disease ([NCT06602258](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06602258)).

## Public sentiment

Scientific discussion reflects cautious optimism. Experts are encouraged by robust biomarker responses but heavily emphasize the risks of severe adverse events and the limitations of an n=8 sample. The broader patient and caregiver communities remain largely unaware of this early-stage asset.

## Caveats

- The extremely small sample size (n=8) limits statistical power and prevents definitive conclusions regarding the drug's safety profile.
- The open-label design lacks a placebo control, making it difficult to confidently attribute specific outcomes directly to the intervention.
