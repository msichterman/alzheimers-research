**Authors**: Darling CC, Sheppard JE, LaCivita CL — FDA Division of Risk Management

## Takeaway

The FDA determined that donanemab (Kisunla) effectively slows clinical decline in early Alzheimer's disease but carries a significant risk of Amyloid Related Imaging Abnormalities (ARIA) and intracerebral hemorrhage. Because prescribers are likely familiar with ARIA protocols from prior anti-amyloid therapies, a formal Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) is unnecessary. Risks will be managed via a boxed warning, medication guides, and enhanced pharmacovigilance.

## Key findings

- Donanemab slowed clinical decline at 76 weeks in the overall population, reducing iADRS decline by 22.3% and CDR-SB decline by 29% (Section 4).
- ARIA (-E or -H) occurred in 36% of donanemab patients compared to 14% on placebo in the pivotal Phase 3 trial (Section 5.2.1).
- Severe radiographic ARIA-H occurred in 22% of ApoE ε4 homozygotes treated with donanemab, compared to 8% of heterozygotes and 4% of noncarriers (Section 5.2.1).
- Five fatal cases related to ARIA were identified in the safety pool, including instances complicated by intracerebral hemorrhage and concurrent thrombolytic (tPA) use (Section 5.2.1).

## Methods in brief

FDA regulatory risk assessment evaluating the 1,727-participant, 76-week placebo-controlled TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 Phase 3 trial alongside an expanded safety pool of 2,802 patients with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease.

## Trial evidence

Donanemab's efficacy and safety were primarily established in the Phase 3 [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2](/trials/trailblazer-alz-2), supported by the Phase 2 [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ](/trials/trailblazer-alz-1). The FDA review pooled safety data across the donanemab program, including [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 4](/trials/trailblazer-alz-4), evaluating ARIA risks that ultimately shaped the drug's 2024 approval.

## Public sentiment

Public perception of donanemab is bifurcated. Patient and caregiver communities express cautious hope for slowing cognitive decline, tempered by significant anxiety over ARIA risks and frustration with Medicare Advantage access hurdles. Concurrently, specialists debate long-term clinical utility and optimal titration protocols to mitigate bleeding events.

## Caveats

- The clinical safety population consisted of relatively healthy patients with mild Alzheimer's disease, potentially underestimating ARIA and hemorrhage risks in older populations or those with advanced comorbidities.
- ApoE ε4 testing is highly recommended to inform ARIA risk before initiating treatment, but an FDA-authorized diagnostic test for ApoE ε4 alleles was not available at the time of the review.

### Cross-examination

3 facts verified against the source document and trial registries. Flagged items:

- **Fact check (corrected)** — N=2802 trial participants. The pivotal Study AACI randomized 1,727 patients. The 2,802 figure represents the expanded all-donanemab safety pool used for long-term safety evaluation.
- **Registry cross-check** — The document reports an 'n' of 2802, while the clinical registry for the pivotal Phase 3 trial (NCT04437511) lists an enrollment of 1736; the larger number in the FDA review represents an expanded safety pool analyzing patients across multiple donanemab trials, rather than solely the primary efficacy cohort of Study AACI.
- **Misinformation watch** — Belief that donanemab reverses or cures Alzheimer's disease rather than just modestly slowing decline.
- **Misinformation watch** — Assumption that the drug is suitable for late-stage or severe Alzheimer's patients (e.g., patients in their 90s with advanced dementia).
