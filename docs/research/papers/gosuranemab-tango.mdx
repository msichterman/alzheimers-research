**Authors**: Biogen

## Takeaway

Gosuranemab (BIIB092), an N-terminal anti-tau monoclonal antibody, failed to slow clinical decline in early Alzheimer's disease compared to placebo. The Phase 2 TANGO trial was terminated for futility after 78 weeks, and Biogen discontinued the drug's development for Alzheimer's.

## Key findings

- Gosuranemab showed no clinical benefit over placebo, with CDR-SB scores worsening by 1.76 to 2.23 points across dose groups compared to a 1.71 worsening for placebo (registry data).
- Adverse event rates were balanced between treatment (82.8% to 88.7%) and placebo (84.6%), with serious adverse events ranging from 10.3% to 12.3% for gosuranemab versus 12.1% for placebo (registry data).
- Scientific post-mortems noted that the highest dose group (2000 mg) experienced accelerated cognitive decline on the ADAS-Cog13 compared to placebo, though this was not included in the top-line registry results.

## Methods in brief

A Phase 2, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial enrolling 654 adults (aged 50-80) with mild cognitive impairment due to AD or mild AD with confirmed amyloid positivity. The trial evaluated multiple doses over a 78-week placebo-controlled period, registered as NCT03352557.

## Trial evidence

The TANGO trial ([NCT03352557](/trials/gosuranemab-nct03352557)) was robustly designed but halted for futility. This failure mirrors the drug's earlier clinical trajectory in another tauopathy; the Phase 2 PASSPORT trial ([NCT03068468](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03068468)) for progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) was similarly halted due to a lack of efficacy, compounding doubts regarding the viability of targeting N-terminal tau.

## Public sentiment

Public response was largely muted among caregivers and retail investors, as the trial's failure in June 2021 was overshadowed by the controversial FDA approval of Biogen's aducanumab in the same month. However, the scientific community critically analyzed the results on platforms like [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/more-tau-antibodies-bid-adieu-semorinemab-keeps-foot-door), expressing concern over the accelerated decline seen in the highest dose group.
