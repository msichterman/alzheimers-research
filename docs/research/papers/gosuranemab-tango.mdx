## Critical review
**Primary endpoint: met.** The document's own primary endpoint was safety and tolerability (the incidence of AEs and SAEs), which the document reports as acceptable and comparable to placebo; the clinical efficacy measure, change in CDR-SB at week 78, was a secondary endpoint and was not met, with no dose separating from placebo.
> Gosuranemab had an acceptable safety profile and was generally well tolerated (incidence of serious adverse events: placebo, 12.1%; low dose, 10.3%; intermediate dose, 12.3%; high dose, 11.7%).
**Multiplicity handling:** None for the reported comparisons: analyses were two-sided at a 5% significance level and no adjustments were made for multiple comparisons. Sample size for the secondary CDR-SB objective was based on the multiple comparison procedure—modeling approach.

### Risk of bias

- **sponsor analysis** — The trial was sponsored and funded by Biogen, the statistical analysis was performed by Biogen statisticians/programmers, and most authors are current or former Biogen employees and shareholders.
- **multiplicity / selective inference** — No adjustment for multiple comparisons was made across the many secondary and exploratory comparisons reported, so nominal P values (including the significant ADAS-Cog13, p-tau181, total tau and lateral ventricle results) are unadjusted.
- **early termination / attrition** — The long-term extension was terminated early for lack of efficacy and no participant completed it, so LTE analyses rest on truncated follow-up.
- **protocol deviations (COVID-19)** — Pandemic-driven scheduling flexibility increased protocol deviations, which the document states were generally balanced across groups.
- **post-hoc analysis of the extension** — The delayed-start / LTE comparisons are labelled post hoc by the document itself and are limited by early termination.
- **overenrollment relative to plan** — Enrollment exceeded the preplanned 528 participants, reaching 654 because of fast recruitment.

### Limitations stated by the document

- Biological/technical limitation: targeting an N-terminal tau epitope may not sufficiently capture the extracellular tau species responsible for mediating tau pathology propagation
- Pathological tau species may spread through pathways not accessible to monoclonal antibodies, such as exosomes or nanotubes, and most tau resides inside neurons
- Preclinical tau transgenic mouse models are unsuitable for testing therapeutics aimed at intercepting extracellular tau, calling into question their predictive value
- The study was affected by the COVID-19 pandemic; visit-scheduling flexibility kept discontinuation low at the expense of increased protocol deviations
- Limited data were collected during the LTE period due to the early termination of the study
- Improved fluid measures are needed to understand the extracellular availability of pathological tau

### Where authoritative sources disagree

*Stored as structured evidence with each position attributed to its source, not resolved into a single verdict.*

- **Does target engagement of extracellular N-terminal tau translate into reduced tau pathology or clinical benefit?** — This document concludes that gosuranemab bound extracellular N-terminal tau robustly but that this engagement produced neither a reduction in tau PET-measured pathology nor clinical benefit. *(The TANGO investigators (Biogen-sponsored study report))*
- **Are tau transgenic mouse models predictive of clinical efficacy for anti-tau antibodies?** — This document argues the predictive value of these preclinical models is questionable, since agents effective in mice have failed clinically. *(The TANGO investigators (Discussion))*
- **Is the N-terminus of tau the right therapeutic epitope?** — This document argues an N-terminal epitope may not capture the extracellular tau species that propagate pathology, and that intracellular tau may be the more important target. *(The TANGO investigators (Discussion))*
- **Have all anti-tau antibody trials in AD been negative?** — This document notes that, in contrast to the negative early-AD trials, semorinemab showed partial efficacy on one coprimary endpoint in moderate AD in the Lauriet trial. *(The TANGO investigators, citing the Lauriet trial of semorinemab)*
