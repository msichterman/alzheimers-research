The amyloid cascade hypothesis proposes that accumulation of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptide in the brain is the initiating event in Alzheimer's disease, with tau pathology, neuroinflammation, and neurodegeneration downstream.

## Supporting evidence

- Mutations in *APP*, *PSEN1*, and *PSEN2* — all in the Aβ production pathway — cause early-onset familial Alzheimer's.
- Trisomy 21 (an extra *APP* copy) leads to near-universal Alzheimer's pathology in Down syndrome.
- Anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) that clear plaques slow clinical decline in early symptomatic disease — the first disease-modifying evidence in humans.

## Open questions

- Plaque burden correlates weakly with cognitive decline; tau tangle spread tracks symptoms better.
- Clinical benefit from amyloid clearance is modest (roughly 25–35% slowing over 18 months in CLARITY AD and TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2). Whether earlier intervention yields larger effects is being tested in prevention trials such as [AHEAD 3-45](/trials/ahead-3-45) and [TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3](/trials/trailblazer-alz-3).
- Soluble Aβ oligomers versus fibrillar plaques as the toxic species remains unresolved.
